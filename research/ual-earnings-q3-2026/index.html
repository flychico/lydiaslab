UAL earnings — United Airlines, Q3 2026

October 20, 202612 min readOpen in the reader

Management sees record revenue and firm pricing ahead, and the one thing that could spoil it is jet fuel rising faster than fares can follow.

Reported 2026-10-20. Next report: not found. Updated 2026-11-12. Research, not investment advice.

The short version

  • Demand: Strong on revenue (up 17.4%, a record), softer in leisure, where load factor fell 1.3 points.
  • Guidance: Cut in effect. Q4 adjusted EPS $0.50 to $1.50 against the $1.76 analysts expected. No full-year figure was given.
  • Biggest worry: Jet fuel. Only about 72% of the increase came back through fares.
  • Chart: No lean. The rebound stalled at $121.87; a daily close above it decides.

The numbers

  • Revenue $17,875M, up 17.4%. Webull's revenue estimate was $17.91B.
  • GAAP EPS $3.14, adjusted EPS $2.54 (a year ago: $2.78). Consensus of $2.57 to $2.60 is an adjusted figure, so compare it with $2.54, a miss by a few cents. Setting the GAAP $3.14 against that consensus mixes two bases and makes it look like a beat. It was not.
  • GAAP EPS was lifted by $294M of sale-leaseback gains. Adjusted EPS landed at the bottom of United's own $2.50 to $3.50 range.

What management said about the road ahead

  • Next quarter: Q4 adjusted EPS $0.50 to $1.50, revenue growth above 20%, fuel about $4.80 a gallon (Oct 16 forward curve). United said analysts assumed about $4.35, and that the $0.45 gap is worth about $1 of EPS.
  • Pricing: Yield up 16.8%, passenger unit revenue up 16.1%. Premium revenue up 17%, Basic Economy up 16%, contracted business revenue up 26%.
  • Fuel recovery: Fuel averaged $3.90 a gallon against $2.43. About 72% was recovered, below the 80% to 90% expected in July. A similar rate is expected in Q4.
  • Leisure: Load factor down 1.3 points, with more softness flagged for Q4.
  • 2027 margin: The double-digit pre-tax margin goal now needs lower fuel, lower industry capacity or continued yield discipline. The non-fuel unit cost target of 2% to 3% growth was reaffirmed.
  • Capacity: Industry capacity is expected to speed up in 2027.
  • Quote: "Our employees met the challenge of our ever-changing environment" (Scott Kirby, CEO, press release).

What management is worried about

  1. Incomplete fuel recovery: about 72% recovered against 80% to 90% planned; Q4 fuel about $4.80 a gallon.
  2. Softer leisure demand: load factor down 1.3 points, more expected in Q4.
  3. Faster industry capacity in 2027: no figure given.
  4. Rising non-fuel costs: a new flight attendant contract; headcount 117.9K, up 5.4%.
  5. FAA limits at Chicago O'Hare from Q2 2027.
  6. The 10-Q risk section was not opened: not found.

The big worry: jet fuel, both ways

What would ease it

  • A real reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. Waller at the Fed says insurance and transfers there add $20 to $30 a barrel.
  • Talks holding. Brent fell to $96.54 on Nov 9 on hopes of de-escalation (ENGINE) and was about $92.5 on Nov 11, down from above $110 in late October.
  • Refineries coming back. Chevron says about 10% of global refining is offline and that margins should ease after staying high near term.
  • Less flying. United's CFO has said marginal routes get cut, and Kirby expects capacity adjustments across the industry in 2027 (CAPA).

What would make it worse

  • More incidents in the Strait. A tanker hit a mine there on Nov 11 and WTI closed near $87.
  • Slow repair of product supply. Exxon's CEO expects normalisation to run well through next year, with clean products stuck in the Middle East and about half of Russia's refining offline.
  • Jet fuel running ahead of crude. Singapore jet averaged $169.53 a barrel in September against $89.03 in February (S&P Global).
  • No help from producers. None of Exxon, Chevron or Occidental is raising spending.

How United is placed against it

  • No hedge found. Reuters (via Hydrocarbon Processing, Mar 6) reported the big US carriers largely stopped hedging. United's own position on a primary page: not found.
  • Pricing power is real but incomplete: yield rose 16.8% and still covered only 72% of fuel.
  • Against Delta: Delta has a refinery benefit of about $0.40 a gallon and guides Q4 fuel near $4.25. United has neither the refinery nor the lower figure.

Money plans

  • Q3 capital spending $2,139M. Full-year guide: not found.
  • Buybacks only $27M in nine months, against $610M a year earlier.
  • Net leverage 2.4x, up from 2.1x. Investment-grade timing slipped to next year at the earliest, by management's own hope.
