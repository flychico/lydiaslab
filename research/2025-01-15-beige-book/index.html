Beige Book — January 15, 2025

January 15, 20252 min readOpen in the reader

Official report: Summary · Full PDF Local PDF: 2025-01-15 Beige Book.pdf

Overall economic activity

Activity rose slightly to moderately in all twelve Districts. Four (Cleveland, Richmond, Atlanta, Dallas) reported moderate growth and eight reported slight growth; none reported a decline. Manufacturing slipped slightly on net, construction fell (high materials and financing costs), and residential real estate was flat because of high mortgage rates. Commercial real estate sales edged up. Nonfinancial services grew slightly, led by leisure, hospitality and air travel, though truck freight volumes fell. Banks reported modest loan growth, but lenders flagged rising delinquencies among small businesses and lower-income households. Agriculture was weak. The report was prepared by the Chicago Fed, and its data predate the Los Angeles wildfires.

Consumer spending

Spending rose moderately. Most Districts said holiday sales beat expectations, and vehicle sales grew modestly.

Labor markets

Employment ticked up: six Districts reported slight gains and six no change. Healthcare and other services kept adding jobs; construction rose slightly and manufacturing was flat. Skilled workers remained hard to find, and layoffs were rare. Wage growth picked up to a moderate pace in most Districts, though some noted easing wage pressure.

Prices

Prices rose modestly overall (ranging from flat to moderate). Most Districts saw modest selling-price increases, but some retail and manufacturing prices were flat or lower. Health insurance premiums were a notable input-cost increase, while fuel costs were flat or lower in places. Contacts expected prices to keep rising in 2025, with tariffs a possible contributor.

Notable themes

  • Tariffs: Some manufacturers were "stockpiling inventories in anticipation of higher tariffs."
  • Policy worries: Several Districts said changes in immigration and tariff policy "could negatively affect the economy."
  • Sentiment: More contacts were optimistic than pessimistic about 2025.
  • Avian flu: Reduced egg supplies and raised egg prices.
  • Housing: Flat, held back by high mortgage rates.

District highlights

  • Cleveland, Richmond, Atlanta, Dallas: Moderate growth; Dallas saw manufacturing growth resume and improved outlooks, though domestic-policy concerns stayed elevated.
  • New York: Holiday retail beat expectations; NYC office demand improved.
  • Philadelphia: Inflation expectations rose over deficits, tariffs and immigration.
  • Chicago: 2024 farm incomes were below 2023 levels.
  • Kansas City: Consumers increasingly used longer-term financing and debt consolidation; contacts expected growth, hiring and price increases to accelerate.
  • Minneapolis: Some holiday sales were above pre-pandemic levels.

Read-through for SPY

  • A broad, if gentle, expansion with strong holiday spending was a supportive backdrop for corporate revenues.
  • Early signs of tariff front-running and rising price expectations hinted at inflation risk that could keep rates higher.
  • Rising delinquencies among lower-income households and small businesses were a watch item for consumer-exposed sectors.
