FOMC Minutes — October 27–28, 2026

October 28, 20265 min readOpen in the reader

Official minutes: HTML · PDF. Released Nov 18, 2026. The breakdown follows.

The one-line read: The Fed held in October but almost every official expects to raise rates again. A couple raised the idea of a hike bigger than a quarter point in December if inflation data disappoint. The new part: AI is now treated as both an inflation risk and a financial stability risk.

How to read the quantifiers the Fed uses (largest to smallest): "almost all" / "vast majority" > "most" > "a majority" > "many" > "a number" / "several" > "some" > "a few" > "a couple" (two). Counting the words is how you measure how strong a view is.

1. The decision

  • Held the federal funds range at 3.75–4.00%. The statement kept the line that the Committee "will deliver price stability."
  • Vote 10–2. Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissented. Both wanted a 0.25-point hike now: inflation too high, few signs of easing, policy not restrictive enough.
  • Interest on reserves stays 3.90%, discount rate 4.0%, reverse repo 3.75%.

2. Where they think rates go next (the part that moves markets)

  • Almost all officials said more hikes, "broadly consistent with the September projections," will likely be appropriate. The September dots already showed one more hike in 2026 (median 4.1%).
  • A couple said a hike of more than 0.25 point could be considered in December if the inflation data disappoint. October core CPI then came in at +0.3% for a third month, which is the kind of reading that keeps a 0.5-point move in play.
  • A few said rates may need to go even higher ("a more restrictive stance") if inflation stays stuck.
  • Some wanted to hike in October already. A few of those said they could live with holding because markets already expected more tightening.
  • Most backed holding. Many wanted more information first: whether AI spending lasts, how financing conditions develop, how September's hike was passing through, and geopolitics. A few called it "a close call."
  • Markets and the Fed agree: futures priced a December hike and two more by summer 2027. The minutes say that alignment lowered the risk of waiting. Dealer survey median: hikes in December and March 2027, a cut in early 2028.

What it means for us: December 9 is a live hike, possibly a big one. The risk is higher short-term rates on top of a 10-year near 5%. That is pressure on small caps (IWM), rate-sensitive names, and high-valuation AI names, and a reason the market keeps losing its 200-day.

3. Inflation: what they see

  • Staff estimates for September: headline PCE 3.6%, core PCE 3.1%. Drivers: energy, earlier tariffs, and AI-related goods prices.
  • Most said inflation pressure is broad-based, not just a few items. Several said tariff effects are fading. The vast majority cited high energy prices.
  • Many said AI demand is straining supply and raising technology goods prices; several said that pressure is spreading beyond data-center industries.
  • The vast majority see inflation risks tilted up, citing energy and tight refined-fuel supply.
  • A majority worried AI costs could pass through more broadly and for longer.
  • Expectations: medium- and long-term still consistent with 2%, but some worried that years above target could get baked in, and a few flagged rising short-term expectations.
  • The other side: a few think AI productivity could eventually lower prices; a number said tougher scrutiny of AI returns could slow spending and cool inflation.

4. AI: now a financial stability topic

  • Almost all officials discussed what happens if AI returns disappoint.
  • Most flagged fast-growing, opaque AI debt; a few worried about concentration in a handful of firms.
  • Several flagged the risk of too much AI investment, and a couple said it could crowd out other investment.
  • Markets during the period: AI stocks rose, then fell after two leading AI firms missed revenue and a hyperscaler needed more financing than expected. Junk bonds funding data centers widened especially notably.
  • Staff: vulnerabilities could amplify shocks "somewhat more than usual." Top concerns: high valuations and hedge fund leverage.

What it means for us: the Fed is saying out loud what the market showed on Nvidia this week: AI is being judged on returns and financing now, not just growth. NVDA, SMH, ORCL (debt-funded buildout) and the AI-capex names carry more downside sensitivity to any financing scare.

5. Labor and growth

  • Unemployment 4.2%; payrolls about 50,000 a month over three months; wages +3% a year and slowing. Officials call it stable, close to maximum employment, with low hiring and rising long-term unemployment.
  • Most say wage growth is consistent with 2% inflation: the labor market is not the problem; prices are.
  • Growth is strong: Q3 picked up, consumer and business spending solid, AI investment robust.
  • Uneven: AI, manufacturing and data centers strong; housing weak (mortgage activity fell further on sharply higher rates). A few said necessities strain lower- and middle-income budgets; a couple flagged diesel costs hitting agriculture.

6. Financial conditions

  • A majority said conditions tightened since September: higher mortgage rates, wider spreads for riskier and AI-related borrowers, less low-rated issuance.
  • Many said borrowing costs weigh on housing, commercial real estate, small business and other rate-sensitive sectors.
  • Bank stocks fell sharply (slower loans, commercial real estate worries); REITs and small caps fell.
  • Credit card delinquencies still elevated; leveraged loan defaults somewhat elevated; commercial mortgage delinquencies elevated.
  • Several said the Fed lacks data on banks' indirect exposure to private credit funds.

7. Markets and the world

  • Treasury yields rose into early October on strong data, then fell back on AI doubts and a French bond scare (France–Germany 10-year spread widest since 2011). Net: little changed.
  • Dollar stronger as other central banks' expected paths moved down while the U.S. path held. The Bank of Japan and Reserve Bank of Australia hiked.
  • Longer-run neutral rate estimate (dealer survey) rose to about 3.4%: the market thinks rates stay higher for longer.

8. Balance sheet

  • Reserves are "ample." Bill purchases to manage reserves stay at zero for now, reviewed monthly. Treasuries roll over at auction; agency principal goes into bills.

9. How this lines up with what the market did on Nov 18

  • 2-year +1bp, 10-year −4bp: the market believes the hikes and expects them to slow growth (a flatter curve).
  • Gold −3.5%, dollar at a 19-month high: real rates rising.
  • Waller the same day: "you have to go 50 in December." Futures about 95% for a December hike.

10. What to watch before December 9

  • Nov 25: October PCE (the Fed's main gauge) and the Beige Book.
  • Dec 4: November jobs report.
  • Dec 10: November CPI (after the meeting).
  • Signals of a 0.5-point hike: core PCE at +0.3% or more, more officials using "more than 25 basis points."
  • Signals against more hikes: an AI financing scare spreading into credit, or a clear break in hiring.
