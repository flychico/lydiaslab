TSLA earnings — Tesla, Q3 2026

October 21, 202613 min readOpen in the reader

Management sees orders ahead of what it can build and a payoff from robotaxis and robots starting in 2027; the thing that could spoil it is that today's profit is thin and leaning on one-off refunds while costs keep rising.

Reported 2026-10-21. Next report: about Jan 27, 2027 (unconfirmed estimate). Updated 2026-11-12. Research, not investment advice.

The short version

  • Demand: steady, limited by battery supply. Revenue was flat; deliveries were 486,532, down 2.1% (EV Wire).
  • Guidance: none on revenue, deliveries or EPS. 2026 capex cut to about $20B from above $25B.
  • Biggest worry: thin core profit. Operating margin 2.6%, and tariff refunds lowered costs by about $786M (my sum), more than the quarter's operating income.
  • Chart: no lean. Price is back above its 200-day ($383.03) but the 50-day is still under it; $414.23 above and $369.59 below decide it.

The numbers

  • Revenue $28.05B (flat) vs $28.24B estimate, a slight miss.
  • GAAP EPS $0.22 vs $0.26 on Webull, a miss. On the adjusted basis Electrek reports $0.48 vs $0.45 consensus, a beat. So the answer depends on the basis; keep the two apart.
  • Operating income about $718M, down 56% from a year ago; regulatory credit revenue $113M, down 73% (Electrek).

What management said about the road ahead

  • Orders: the passenger-vehicle order backlog is the largest in four years (via Electrek); volumes were held back by battery supply, not orders.
  • Energy storage: 13.7 GWh deployed; Megapack output almost sold out for 2027.
  • Margins: the 33% energy margin was helped by tariff refunds and a supplier reimbursement. Refunds cut costs by $526M in energy and $260M in automotive.
  • Costs: operating expenses up 39%, and expected to keep rising until new products sell.
  • Robotaxi and self-driving: operating in six cities; the cost-per-mile target is not yet met. Targets: first Cybercab customer deliveries by the end of 2026, unsupervised self-driving on customer cars around Q2 2027, robotaxi revenue noticeable in Q2 2027. On Europe-wide approval: "I would be surprised if it takes longer than Q1" (Elon Musk, CEO; third-party transcript).
  • Optimus: outside deliveries around the end of 2027.
  • Spending: no 2027 figure; a chip plant growing to tens of billions in 2028.

What management is worried about

  1. Battery supply. The shareholder letter calls battery availability the limiting factor on car volumes.
  2. Rising expenses ahead of revenue. Operating expenses +39% with revenue flat.
  3. A bumpy Optimus ramp and timelines that depend on regulators.
  4. Commodity prices and interest rates. In September Tesla signed $30B of undrawn credit.
  5. Governments restricting operations.
  6. Filing risk factors: the 10-Q adds none and points back to the annual report.

The big worry: thin profit while spending climbs, both ways

What would ease it

  • More batteries. With the largest order backlog in four years, each extra pack is a car sold.
  • Energy storage holding its margin without refunds. Megapack is almost sold out for 2027, and the AI builders you track are all short of power.
  • Self-driving approvals landing on time: unsupervised driving around Q2 2027 and a Europe decision.
  • Capital discipline. The 2026 capex plan was cut by more than $5B.

What would make it worse

  • The refunds ending. Apple's CFO said the vast majority of its tariff refunds are already in; if Tesla's are similar, the roughly $786M of help does not repeat.
  • Regulators. NHTSA upgraded its self-driving probe to an engineering analysis in March 2026, covering about 3.2M vehicles (CBT News); a petition on robotaxis speeding in school zones was opened Oct 9 (Automotive World); the EU committee delayed its vote, with a decision in December at the earliest (Vision Mobility).
  • Higher rates. The Fed Desk has a December hike about 90% priced and the 10-year near 5%; that raises the cost of car loans and of Tesla's $30B standby credit if it is drawn.
  • Regulatory credit revenue continuing to shrink (down 73% in a year).
  • Memory and chip costs. The chip makers you track all report memory prices rising, and a car is now a computer too; Tesla did not give a figure.

How this company is placed against it

  • Cash is not the near-term problem: the $30B of credit is undrawn, and capex was trimmed.
  • The car business is not paying for the new ones. Nine-month capex was $13.0B against $6.1B a year earlier, on a 2.6% operating margin.
  • The SpaceX tie cuts both ways. SpaceX is spending $35–40B in one quarter on AI capacity and is growing fast; Tesla holds SpaceX stock carried at $2.87B, sold it $221M of goods in the quarter, and both boards have special committees studying a combination, with no terms agreed. Alphabet's quarter shows how much SpaceX shares can swing a holder's earnings.

Money plans

  • Capex $13.0B in nine months; 2026 plan about $20B (the 10-Q says in excess of $20B). Quarterly capex of about $4.7B appears only in press coverage.
  • $30B of undrawn credit signed in September: a $20B term loan and $10B of revolving facilities.
  • No buybacks or dividend found. Headcount: not found.

People and governance

  • CEO: Elon Musk, CEO since October 2008. He also leads SpaceX, whose filing refers to a controlling shareholder.
  • Chair: Robyn Denholm, since November 2018. The roles are split.
  • CFO: Vaibhav Taneja, since August 2023.
  • Successor named: no. The proxy says a board committee reviews succession regularly.
  • Control: Musk owns about 20% (Electrek); no dual-class shares found. The board is staggered and supermajority rules remain.
  • Pay: the 2025 CEO award is valued at $132.3B in 12 tranches; Tesla rates only one operating milestone as probable. Tranches can vest on a change of control if the market-value test is met, which matters if a SpaceX deal happens. Musk gave up a $26.1B interim award in April 2026 after the Delaware Supreme Court reinstated his 2018 package (Electrek).
  • Recent changes or investigations: both Tesla and SpaceX formed independent special committees on a combination; Musk is recused, and a Tesla merger needs approval from half of all shares outstanding (TechCrunch). Reuters, via the Korea Times, reports the talks are early. In a securities case over his 2022 Twitter purchase there was a partial judgment against Musk in April 2026; an appeal is pending (proxy). The head of energy and charging stepped down in October, per a Wall Street Journal report relayed by tech-insider; Tesla has not confirmed it. Annual meeting: Dec 3, 2026.
  • How much rests on one person: nearly everything. The timelines, the pay plan, the SpaceX talks and the valuation all run through Musk, and he sits on both sides of the possible deal.
