BE earnings — Bloom Energy, Q3 2026

October 22, 202614 min readOpen in the reader

Management sees demand for on-site power spreading well beyond the biggest data center builders, and the thing that could spoil it is that sales rest on very few customers whose projects need permits and financing.

Reported 2026-10-22. Next report: not found. Updated 2026-11-12. Research, not investment advice.

The short version

  • Demand: stronger. Revenue up 138.1%; backlog is growing faster than revenue (the figure comes with the Q4 report).
  • Guidance: raised, for the third time this year. 2026 revenue $4.4B–$4.6B, from $3.9B–$4.2B; non-GAAP EPS $3.10–$3.40, from $2.55–$2.85. Gross margin held at about 34%.
  • Biggest worry: a few customers, whose sites need permits and money. Two customers were about 43% and 26% of revenue.
  • Chart: no lean. Price closed $209.47, between your 0.786 ext $156.28 (the floor that held) and your 1.382 ext $227.97. A 3-day close above $227.97 decides it up.

The numbers

  • Revenue $1,235.8M, up 138.1%, vs $1,071.7M estimated. Product revenue $1,073.6M.
  • GAAP EPS $0.78 vs $0.54 estimated; non-GAAP EPS $0.94. The estimate is Webull's and analysts usually estimate non-GAAP, so this EPS comparison mixes GAAP and adjusted. Treat it as rough.
  • Non-GAAP gross margin 34.5%. Operations used $27.1M of cash in the quarter.

What management said about the road ahead

  • Full year: revenue $4.4B–$4.6B; non-GAAP operating income $960M–$1,030M, from $800M–$900M. No 2027 figures.
  • Backlog: growing faster than revenue. No dollar figure until the Q4 report.
  • Year-end: where 2026 lands depends on a small number of year-end deliveries and whether customer sites are ready.
  • Pricing: holding.
  • Margins: new production lines and rushed deliveries are a near-term drag; the guide stays about 34%.
  • New channel: utilities, including a 700MW project announced by AEP to serve Google.
  • Supply: the 10-Q says component supply has not been affected.
  • In their words: "What began with hyperscalers is rapidly moving across market segments." (KR Sridhar, Founder, Chairman and CEO)

What management is worried about

  1. Customer concentration. Two customers were about 43% and about 26% of revenue; the second is a related party.
  2. Opposition to data centers. The 10-Q points to growing opposition, including a July 2026 New York moratorium order.
  3. Tighter financing for data centers. Named in the 10-Q's discussion section.
  4. Timing. A small number of year-end deliveries, and customer sites being ready, decide the year.
  5. Tariffs and tax credits. New tariffs, and reliance on tax credits.
  6. The short-seller report and shareholder lawsuit. See People and governance.
  7. The 10-Q states no material changes to its Risk Factors.

The big worry: a few customers who need permits and financing, both ways

What would ease it

  • More buyers. The utility channel (AEP's 700MW project) and the spread beyond the largest builders would dilute the two big customers.
  • A backlog figure with the Q4 report that shows many names, not two.
  • New York's pause ends on schedule. The governor's office says up to one year from July 14, 2026, ending when the state sets its standards (Jones Day).
  • The Fed stops hiking, which loosens data center credit.
  • Customer deposits keep rising (up $583M this year), which means buyers are paying ahead.

What would make it worse

  • Other states copy New York. Its order pauses discretionary state permits to build or expand data centers and, per Jones Day, sets no hard one-year cap.
  • Credit tightens further: about 90% odds of a December hike, the 10-year near 5%, and Governor Bowman flagging over $800B of AI debt.
  • One of the two big customers delays a site. At 43% or 26% of revenue, a single slip moves the year.
  • Tax credits are cut, or tariffs lift costs while margin is already under pressure from new lines.

How this company is placed against it

  • For it: the product exists because the grid cannot connect data centers fast enough, pricing is holding, and cash of about $2.49B roughly matches recourse debt of about $2.5B.
  • Against it: purchase commitments of about $5.8B and capital spending about four times last year's are being made ahead of a backlog you cannot yet see. Operations used cash in the quarter.
  • Versus peers in this group: Bloom is the most concentrated. Super Micro's top customer is 44% of sales; Bloom's top two are about 69%.

Money plans

  • Capital spending: $153M in the quarter, $231M this year, about four times all of 2025. Preparing capacity beyond the earlier 5-gigawatt target, in Fremont and Delaware; no timeline.
  • Debt and cash: cash about $2.49B; recourse debt about $2.5B.
  • Commitments: purchase commitments about $5.8B.
  • Buybacks and dividend: none found.

People and governance

  • CEO: KR Sridhar, founder, CEO and chairman since 2002 (2026 proxy). All three roles in one person.
  • CFO: Simon Edwards, per the lawsuit coverage. His start date: not found. The same coverage names a former CFO (Daniel Berenbaum) and a former acting finance chief (Maciej Kurzymski), so the finance seat changed hands more than once inside the suit's Feb 2025 to Jul 2026 window.
  • Successor named: no. The proxy speaks of leadership continuity and of the importance of retaining Sridhar; no successor is named.
  • Board: lead independent director is Jeffrey Immelt. Directors serve staggered three-year terms. In May 2026 holders approved shielding certain officers from some liability claims, as Delaware allows.
  • Control: a single class of stock, one vote per share; the old Class B wording was removed in May 2026. Holders above 5%: not found (ownership table not read).
  • Related party: the customer at about 26% of revenue is a related party (10-Q). Its name: not found in the pages read.
  • Investigations and lawsuits: Bloomberg Law reported that Hunterbrook Media published a report on July 8, 2026 alleging Bloom hid a reliance on Chinese scandium routed through other countries, and that an investor filed a proposed class action on July 30 in federal court in Northern California. TipRanks reported the suit names Sridhar, Edwards, Kurzymski and Berenbaum, and that the complaint cites earlier company statements that it was not dependent on China. These are allegations; nothing has been decided. A regulator inquiry: not found.
  • How much rests on one person: a great deal. The founder has held every top job for 24 years with no successor named, the finance seat has changed hands, and a quarter of sales goes to a related party. Of the companies you track in this group, this is the one most exposed to a single person.
