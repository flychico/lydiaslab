1. The Fed minutes: almost everyone wants another hike, and AI is now a stability worry
My read: December 9 is a live hike and a half-point move is on the table. I'm fairly sure of the direction (the Fed's own words are lopsided), less sure of the size. What would change my mind: a soft October PCE on Nov 25 or a credit scare tied to AI debt.
The decision they were explaining (Oct 27–28): held the funds rate at 3.75–4.00%. The vote was 10–2: Governor Cook and Cleveland's Hammack dissented because they wanted a 0.25-point hike right then. Inflation too high, policy not tight enough.
How to read the Fed's words. The minutes never name names; they count. From most to fewest: "almost all" > "most" > "many" > "several" > "some" > "a few" > "a couple" (two people). Counting the words is how you measure how strong a view is.

Inflation. Staff put September headline PCE at 3.6% and core at 3.1%. Most officials called the pressure broad-based, not a few items. New this time: many said AI demand is pushing up technology goods prices, and several said that's spreading beyond data centers. Tariff effects are fading. October core CPI then came in at +0.3% for a third month, the kind of number that keeps the half-point in play.
AI as a stability risk. Almost all officials discussed what happens if AI returns disappoint. Most flagged fast-growing, opaque AI debt; a few worried about concentration in a handful of firms; a couple said AI spending could crowd out other investment. Junk bonds that fund data centers widened "especially notably." Staff said today's weak spots could amplify a shock "somewhat more than usual."
Jobs and growth. Unemployment 4.2%, payrolls about 50,000 a month, wages +3% and slowing. Most said wages aren't the inflation problem; prices are. Growth is strong but uneven: AI, data centers and manufacturing hot, housing weak.
Financial conditions. A majority said conditions tightened since September: higher mortgage rates, wider spreads for risky and AI borrowers. Bank stocks, REITs and small caps fell in the period. Several said the Fed can't see banks' exposure to private credit funds.
Balance sheet. Reserves "ample"; bill purchases stay at zero, reviewed monthly. No change.
How the market took it. The 2-year rose 1bp to 4.80% while the 10-year fell 4bp to 5.07%. Gold fell 3.5% and the dollar hit a 19-month high. Waller on CNBC the same day: "you have to go 50 in December." Futures put about 95% odds on a Dec 9 hike. A rising front end with a falling long end means the market believes the hikes and expects them to slow the economy later.
What it means for our trades. High-valuation AI names (NVDA, SMH, ORCL with its debt-funded buildout) carry more downside on any financing scare: that supports the NVDA put idea. Small caps (IWM) and rate-sensitive names stay heavy. It's also why I'm not shorting TLT just because the Fed sounds tough: the long end is rallying on hike talk.
Dates before Dec 9: Nov 25 October PCE and the Beige Book · Dec 4 November jobs · Dec 10 November CPI (after the meeting). A half-point gets likelier if core PCE prints +0.3% or more. Saved: the full breakdown is in the vault as "2026-10-28 FOMC Minutes".

Lesson of the day
What a flattening yield curve is telling you

The yield curve is the line from short-term to long-term Treasury yields. Short yields follow what the Fed does now; long yields follow what the market expects for growth and inflation over years.

Today the 2-year rose 1bp to 4.80% while the 10-year fell 4bp to 5.07%. The gap between them shrank from about 32bp to 27bp. That is a flattening: the market believes the Fed's hikes, and it believes they will slow the economy later. That is why TLT, the long-bond fund, rose 0.8% on a day the minutes talked about bigger hikes.

Why it matters to us: a flattening on hawkish news is a late-cycle tell. It favors strong balance sheets and steady earners (AAPL, KO, AMZN's cash flows) over stocks that need cheap money, and it is a reason not to buy TLT puts just because the Fed sounds tough. If the curve steepens instead, with long yields jumping, it means inflation fear, and that hits everything.

2-yr minus 10-yr: 27bp (down from about 32bp) · TLT: +0.8% (long bonds rallied on hike talk)
