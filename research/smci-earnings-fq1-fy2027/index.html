SMCI earnings — Super Micro Computer, fiscal Q1 FY2027

November 3, 202614 min readOpen in the reader

Management sees more AI server orders than it can fill and raised its sales target for the year, but it keeps very little of each sales dollar and nearly half of those dollars come from one customer.

Reported 2026-11-03. Next report: not found. Updated 2026-11-12. Research, not investment advice.

The short version

  • Demand: stronger. Sales of $19.9B against a guide of $14.5B–$15.5B; backlog called a record, with no dollar figure.
  • Guidance: raised. Fiscal 2027 sales $72B–$82B, from $65B–$72B. Gross margin guided lower: 11.8%–12.2% for the year, 12.0%–12.5% next quarter.
  • Biggest worry: thin margin resting on one customer (44% of sales, 12.7% gross margin, cash going out).
  • Chart: leans down. It turns neutral on a close back above the 200-day at $32.58; a close under $25.81 confirms the downside.

The numbers

  • Net sales $19.9B (year ago $5.0B) vs $16.3B estimate. About $2.3B of that was catch-up from deployments scheduled for earlier periods, so the clean figure is nearer $17.6B.
  • GAAP EPS $1.75 vs $1.25 estimate; non-GAAP EPS $1.81. The estimate is Webull's and analysts usually estimate non-GAAP, so the EPS beat mixes the two. Treat it as rough.
  • Gross margin 12.7%, down from 17.5% the quarter before. Operations used $434M of cash while sales quadrupled.

What management said about the road ahead

  • Next quarter: sales $19.5B–$21.5B, GAAP EPS $1.59–$1.83, gross margin 12.0%–12.5%. That is flat sales and a lower margin.
  • Backlog: a record, but no number. You cannot size it from what they gave.
  • Supply: demand is above the supply of AI chips and other parts. Customers' sites (land, power, buildings) are also a limit.
  • Margins: settling around 12%. AI systems were about 71% of sales and are headed for the upper end of 70%–80%, which weighs on margin. The second half is guided lower than the first.
  • Pricing: rising component costs are lifting selling prices, but the second-half outlook does not assume much more of that.
  • Customers: concentration is expected to stay high while the large build-outs run.
  • Capacity: target of more than 8,000 racks a month by December 2026. "Supermicro's transformation into a Total AI and IT Datacenter Solution provider continues to accelerate." Charles Liang, Founder, President and CEO.

What management is worried about

  1. One customer: 44% of net sales in the quarter. Two customers were 49.0% and 16.8% of receivables (10-Q).
  2. Margin: gross margin 12.7%, guided to 11.8%–12.2% for the year and lower in the second half.
  3. Cash and funding: operations used $434M; inventory rose to $15.1B; cash $6.9B against debt of $8.6B; credit lines fully drawn; purchase commitments $33.3B.
  4. Controls: disclosure controls judged not effective. The weakness in IT access controls, first reported in the 10-K, was still open on Sep 30.
  5. Legal: shareholder and derivative lawsuits, two SEC subpoenas (Nov 19, 2024 and Apr 28, 2026) and a grand jury subpoena tied to the export-control case. The company says it cannot estimate a loss.
  6. Related-party suppliers: two companies run by the CEO's brothers supply most chassis and power supplies.

The big worry: thin margin on one customer, both ways

What would ease it

  • A second and third large customer taking real share, so the 44% figure falls while sales hold.
  • Gross margin holding at or above the 12.0%–12.5% guide for two quarters in a row.
  • Operating cash flow turning positive as the $15.1B of inventory ships and gets paid for.
  • Chip supply loosening. Nvidia says supply stays the bottleneck through its fiscal 2028, so this is not close.
  • The controls weakness closed and the subpoenas resolved without a charge against the company.

What would make it worse

  • The big customer delaying a site or moving orders to a rival. Dell exited its July quarter with a $95B AI server backlog and a 20.9% gross margin, so it can afford to fight on price.
  • Memory and parts costs rising faster than SMCI can pass them on. Micron expects memory supply to be tighter in 2027 and 2028 than in 2026. TrendForce (Oct 20) sees DRAM contract prices up about 20% this quarter, after about 40% and about 80% in the two quarters before.
  • Higher borrowing costs. The Fed held at 3¾–4% and futures show about 90% odds of a December hike (Fed Desk). SMCI's term loans and revolvers were $3,977.1M at rates of 1.3% to 5.8% (10-Q).
  • A need to sell stock. The CFO said there are no plans to, but a $1.25B at-the-market program is in place and unused (10-Q).
  • A slow-paying customer. With 49.0% of receivables in one name, one late payment is a funding problem.

How this company is placed against it

  • Weakly on price: about 12 cents of gross profit per sales dollar leaves little room. Dell keeps about 21 cents.
  • Tight on funding: cash $6.9B against debt of $8.6B, credit lines fully drawn, $33.3B of purchase commitments, and convertible debt of $4,725.0M (10-Q).
  • Its strength is speed and volume: it shipped $4.4B more than the top of its own guide. That is why the customer is there. It is not a moat on margin.

Money plans

  • Capital spending: $47M in the quarter; $70M–$80M guided for next quarter. Small for a company this size.
  • Debt: $8.6B against $6.9B of cash. Credit lines are fully drawn.
  • Shares: common stock and mandatory convertible preferred were issued in June 2026; a $1.25B at-the-market program had no sales as of Sep 30 (10-Q). The CFO said no new share sale is planned.
  • Buybacks and dividend: not found.

People and governance

  • CEO: Charles Liang, founder. President, CEO and Chairman since the company began in September 1993. One person holds both jobs; the proxy says that suits a small board.
  • Lead independent director: Scott Angel, named January 2026 for a one-year term.
  • CFO: changed on earnings day. Mark Long (former Western Digital CFO) was named CFO on Nov 3, 2026, effective immediately. David Weigand, CFO since February 2021, stays to help the handover and moves to another role.
  • Successor named: no. The proxy only says a board committee reviews succession periodically.
  • Control: one share class found. Liang and his wife Sara Liu (a co-founder and director) held 13.4% as of Jan 31, 2026. That is influence, not control.
  • Recent changes and investigations:
    • Mar 19, 2026: federal prosecutors in New York unsealed an indictment of co-founder and director Yih-Shyan Wally Liaw, a Taiwan sales manager and a contractor, alleging at least $2.5B of AI servers were diverted to China (as reported by Al Jazeera). The company was not charged and says it is cooperating.
    • Mar 20, 2026: Liaw resigned from the board; the two employees were put on leave; the contractor was cut off; an acting Chief Compliance Officer (DeAnna Luna) was named (8-K). Compliance had sat under the CFO.
    • Apr 15, 2026 annual meeting: director Sherman Tuan was re-elected with 172.3M votes for and 108.0M withheld. That is a large protest vote.
    • Auditor: BDO USA. The proxy notes a change of auditors in fiscal 2025; the earlier firm's name: not found in the pages read.
    • Related parties: Ablecom is run by Liang's brother Steve; Liang and his wife own about 10.5% of it. Compuware is run by another brother, Bill. SMCI bought $197.7M from the two in the quarter and gave Compuware a $200M trade credit line in November 2025 (10-Q).
  • How much rests on one person: a great deal. Founder, chairman and CEO for 33 years, family ties to key suppliers, no named successor, a new CFO, and an open controls weakness. This is the highest people risk in your chips group.
