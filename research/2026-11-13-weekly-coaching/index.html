Lesson of the week: Position sizing: the $800 cap is a ceiling, not a target

Position sizing means deciding how much one trade is allowed to lose before you buy it. With a long option, the most you can lose is what you paid, so the premium is the risk. This week showed why that matters.

  • DAL $90 call: −59% ($632.50 → $261.50)
  • DAL stock: −6.5% ($92.25 → $86.25)
  • 3 open plays: −$556 (paper, all at once)
  • 2 closed plays: +$806.50 (banked)

A 6.5% dip in DAL took 59% off the call: leverage works both ways. Many professionals risk 1–2% of the account per idea. Example: on a $25,000 account, 2% is $500, so one $600 call with a stop that would cost about $400 fits; three full-size calls that can all fall on the same day do not. Our rule from here: before entering, add up what every open play would lose if each hit its stop tomorrow. If that total is more than about 5% of the account, the next play waits or gets smaller.

Shallow pullbacks: Visa dipped 18.7% of its run and the buyers were waiting

Last week's chart first. The PLTR extension (A $106.37, B $212.94, C $152.60) didn't come in, so here are the answers: 0.5 = $205.89, 0.786 = $236.36, 1.382 = $299.88, 1.618 = $325.03. PLTR stayed far below them; what mattered was its 50% retracement ($159.65), which held with a $161.00 low.

The idea. In a strong trend, pullbacks are shallow. Buyers who missed the run don't wait for the 50% or 61.8%: they buy the first dip, so the drop stops in the 23.6–38.2% zone. A pullback that holds there says the trend is healthy. One that slices through 38.2% and keeps going says it's tiring.

The swing. Visa's Jun 3 low $307.86 (where this leg began) to the Nov 5 high $441.41 (the top before this pullback). Range: $133.55.

  • 23.6% = 441.41 − 133.55 × 0.236 = 441.41 − 31.52 = $409.89
  • 38.2% = 441.41 − 133.55 × 0.382 = 441.41 − 51.02 = $390.39
  • 50% = 441.41 − 66.78 = $374.64
  • Depth of this pullback = (441.41 − 416.43) ÷ 133.55 = 18.7%
