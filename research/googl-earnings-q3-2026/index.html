GOOGL earnings — Alphabet, Q3 2026

October 28, 202613 min readOpen in the reader

Management sees cloud demand it cannot fully serve and plans to spend much more next year, and the thing that could spoil it is the bill: a company that used to buy back its stock is now selling shares and bonds to build.

Reported 2026-10-28. Next report: not found (Webull lists no date yet; management promised 2027 spending detail on the Q4 call). Updated 2026-11-12. Research, not investment advice.

The short version

  • Demand: stronger. Google Cloud revenue +103%; backlog $560.8B, up from $514B in July.
  • Guidance: none on revenue or EPS, as usual. 2026 capital spending held at $195–205B; 2027 to rise "significantly", figure due on the Q4 call.
  • Biggest worry: paying for the build. Free cash flow was negative $9.1B in the quarter and buybacks have stopped.
  • Chart: leans down. Price $293.45 is under all three averages; the Oct 30 low at $276.92 is the floor that decides it.

The numbers

  • Revenue $127.58B (+25%) against a $127.97B estimate: a slight miss.
  • GAAP EPS $2.12 against a $3.01 estimate. The miss is an $8.7B loss on equity holdings, which the filing ties largely to unrealised losses on SpaceX shares. It is a mark on investments, not the business.
  • Operating income rose about 30% to $40.6B. Cloud operating margin 36.2% (23.7% a year ago).

What management said about the road ahead

  • Cloud demand: revenue $30.7B. Part of the growth is sales of its own AI chip systems as hardware; growth without those was not given.
  • Backlog: $560.8B, just over 55% turning into revenue within 24 months. It includes a $16.8B agreement with a joint venture Alphabet partly owns.
  • Supply: Cloud is short of capacity and has started renting third-party compute.
  • Margins: "We expect margin headwinds in the near-term as we utilize this capacity." (Anat Ashkenazi, CFO)
  • Search: revenue +12%, with query growth the strongest in about five years. AI Mode queries earn less per query for now.
  • Next quarter: larger currency headwinds and a tougher Search comparison.
  • Spending: 2027 capital spending rises significantly. A figure above $300B has appeared in press reports and analyst notes (IFR attributes it to analysts); it was not in the company transcript and is unverified.

What management is worried about

  1. Capacity limits. Cloud cannot meet demand from its own buildings, so it is renting compute at lower margin.
  2. Cost of the build. Capital spending $51.4B in the quarter, $132.0B year to date; depreciation and energy costs rising.
  3. Cloud margin. Guided to face near-term pressure from the rented capacity, after reaching 36.2%.
  4. AI Mode earning less. Search is growing, but the new format makes less per query today.
  5. Swings in investment values. The $8.7B loss this quarter moved EPS by far more than the business did.
  6. Legal. EU fines with a $1.0B charge this quarter; the search judgment is under appeal by both sides.

The big worry: paying for the build, both ways

What would ease it

  • A 2027 spending figure on the Q4 call that comes with a funding plan leaning on cash flow, not new share sales.
  • Backlog converting on schedule: more than half of $560.8B is due within 24 months.
  • Lower long-term rates. The 10-year Treasury is near 5%, and every new bond is priced off it.
  • Its own chips doing more of the work, which lowers the cost of each unit of capacity and brings in hardware revenue.

What would make it worse

  • A 2027 number near or above $300B with another large equity raise. June's offerings reached about $90B, including convertible preferred paying 6.25% (Cleary Gottlieb; IFR).
  • Bond buyers asking for more. Alphabet paid a "large concession" on its $25B August sale, and AI-issuer spreads ran about 115 basis points against 78 for the investment-grade index (BNN Bloomberg, Sept 22).
  • A hiking Fed. Futures put about 90% odds on a December hike, and Fed Vice Chair for Supervision Bowman has flagged AI debt as a risk.
  • Memory and energy costs rising faster than Cloud prices. Micron expects memory to be tighter in 2027 and 2028.
  • More equity marks: the SpaceX stake can cut reported EPS again.

How this company is placed against it

  • Strong credit: S&P AA+ with a stable outlook (The DESK, May 27). Moody's warned in July on hyperscaler credit quality without a rating action (FinanceBuzz, third-party).
  • The balance sheet has changed character in one year: long-term debt $125.1B against $46.5B at year-end, plus $30.5B of common stock and $19.0B of convertible preferred sold.
  • Against peers: Microsoft is building from cash flow with no new debt. Alphabet sits with Amazon and Meta among the builders now relying on outside money, though its operating income ($40.6B a quarter) is large.

Money plans

  • Capital spending: $195–205B for 2026 (held); 2027 significantly higher. Purchase commitments $924.4B; leases not yet started $130.5B.
  • Buybacks: none in 2026 ($39.8B in the same nine months of 2025).
  • Dividend: $0.22 a share.
  • Debt and equity: about $81B of notes year to date, $28.7B of it in Q3; ongoing at-the-market share sales.

People and governance

  • CEO: Sundar Pichai, CEO of Google since 2015 and of Alphabet since 2019. Not a founder.
  • CFO: Anat Ashkenazi, since July 2024.
  • Successor named: no. Press speculation only.
  • Control: dual-class. Founders Larry Page and Sergey Brin hold about 52.7% of the votes between them (27.4% and 25.3%, 2026 proxy). The chair is separate and independent: John Hennessy, since 2018.
  • Recent changes: in August 2026 Demis Hassabis stepped down as head of Google DeepMind and stayed as its chair and Alphabet's chief scientist; Koray Kavukcuoglu now runs it. Jeff Dean left to start a company. The same report says two other senior researchers "reportedly" moved to rival labs (Business Today, Aug 6). Investigations of executives: not found.
  • How much rests on one person: less on the CEO than on two founders. Page and Brin can outvote everyone else, so shareholders cannot force a change of course on spending. The loss of several top AI scientists in one year is the people risk to watch.
