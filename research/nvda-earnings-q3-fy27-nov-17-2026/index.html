NVDA earnings — Nvidia, Q3 FY27

November 17, 202637 min readOpen in the reader

Management sees demand outrunning supply into fiscal 2028; what could spoil it is that Nvidia is now financing a growing share of its own customers while the CEO chair sits empty.

Reported Nov 17, 2026 (quarter ended Oct 25, 2026). Next report: late February 2027 (date not found). Updated Nov 18, 2026. Research, not investment advice.

After-hours prices: Webull, 8 pm and about 11 pm ET on Nov 17. Option values for the Nov 18 open are model estimates.

The short version

  • Demand: stronger than anyone modeled. Revenue $115.8B against a $108B guide; Rubin did close to $20B in its first production quarter; the cloud backlog was put at "$2.5 trillion".
  • Guidance: raised. Q4 revenue $130.0B ±3% vs about $125.25B expected; gross margin 71–72% (the bottom they already flagged); fiscal 2028 growth view up to about 75% from about 70%.
  • Biggest worry: Nvidia is now financing its own buildout. Guarantees rose to $118.0B (from $108.5B), supply commitments to $390B, total future commitments $518B, and receivables stretched to 64 days.
  • Chart: leans down, with one live bounce. Your 0.5 ($155.33) held on the 2-day close again; the 2-day still has eight lower highs in a row. The line that decides it is $170: your 0.786 ($170.22), the Nov 13 high and the straddle's upper breakeven all sit there.

2. The guide is the stock

The quarter tells you what happened. The guide tells you what you're paying for. Q4 at $130.0B is $4.75B (3.8%) above the published Street number. But the published number wasn't the bar. Solus's Dan Greenhouse said the buy side wanted "guidance of $130 billion", and UBS expected about the same. So the guide met the whisper, it didn't beat it. Q4 also has a 14th week, so part of that $130B is an extra week of sales. History says that matters. The four reports from Aug 2025 to May 2026 were all beats, and the stock fell the next day every time (−0.4% to −5.5%). The one that broke the pattern, Aug 26, guided $108B against $103.9B, a 4% clear of the whisper, and rose about 7–9% the next day. Tonight looks more like the first group: the stock was +4.8% at the 8 pm after-hours close ($162.90) and +1.0% ($156.94) on the overnight venue near 11 pm. Nvidia beat by $7 billion, and by midnight the market had given back four-fifths of the pop. The margin guide (71–72%) is exactly the bottom they flagged in August, with 72–73% for fiscal 2028. No surprise there, good or bad. The raise to about 75% growth for fiscal 2028 is the real new information, and Goldman leaned on it tonight.

3. What management said about the road ahead

  • Who spoke: Colette Kress, now interim CEO and still CFO, ran the call; chair Harvey Jones opened on the leadership change.
  • Rubin: "off to an explosive start", about $20B in its first production quarter, which they called the fastest ramp in their history. Vera Rubin Ultra is "on track" for next year, Feynman for 2028. Blackwell Ultra got credit for the year-on-year margin gain; the quarter-on-quarter dip is the early Rubin ramp.
  • Demand visibility: the cloud backlog is "$2.5 trillion", up from $2 trillion three months ago. Kress: demand is "continuing to outpace supply". B300 rental rates are up more than 60%, so the older chips are not being dumped.
  • Supply limits: wafers, substrates and advanced packaging, with new supplier deals (Amkor, Samsung Electro-Mechanics, SPIL). Kress did not name HBM memory or CoWoS on the call, but the $390B of supply commitments are mostly multi-year memory buying, so memory is where the money is going.
  • Power: "land, power, and shell" is "such a critical part". Customer sites running late is now a named risk.
  • Sovereign and enterprise: sovereign AI revenue more than tripled from a year ago; enterprise is expected to more than double in fiscal 2028, led by financial services. Australia: up to 2GW of capacity by 2027.
  • China: Q3 included $2.4B of licensed Hopper sales; "we are no longer shipping" them, and Q4 assumes zero China data center compute. New Commerce rules landed this week; the board formed an export-control oversight committee.
  • Financing: guarantees are $118.0B, including $105.0B of credit support for SB Energy campuses leased to OpenAI for up to 20 years. No payments made so far. Kress: they will be "disciplined and selective" and will "remain thoughtful in how we participate". Priority order for cash: supplier prepayments, then selective customer support, then buybacks.
  • Money plans: $28.1B returned in the quarter (a record), $150B added to the buyback ($227.3B left, to be used by end of fiscal 2028), dividend $0.25. Nvidia also agreed to buy Hugging Face.

4. What they worry about, and what they dodged

  • 1. Site readiness and power. Customer data centers that are "delayed or they have a challenge in terms of building that". This is the first time it's a named drag, and the post-election state moratoriums make it worse.
  • 2. Supply cost. "a significant change in terms of the cost of supply". That's memory. It's why Q4 margin steps down to 71–72% even as revenue jumps $14B.
  • 3. The balance sheet doing the selling. $118.0B of guarantees, $32B of neocloud commitments, $20B of equity commitments to labs and financiers, inventory up to $39.7B from $31.6B, and receivables at 64 days (60 last quarter). A $1.8B impairment on private holdings shows some of those bets are already marked down.
  • 4. Export rules and China. Zero is in the guide, so it can only surprise up, but the new rules are unpriced.
  • 5. Leadership and the investigation, which the release now lists as a forward-looking risk.

What they dodged. The board said up front it would not take questions on Huang's exit, and his name was never said on the call. UBS asked about long-term pricing against custom chips: "something for the future". Cantor asked how far vendor financing can go: no limit given. Goldman asked for hurdle rates on that financing: none given. Morgan Stanley asked whether the "100% demand" framing still holds: not answered directly. Nobody addressed the roughly $500B chip-financing platform that was reported to be in the works with Goldman. The things they wouldn't size are exactly the things a bear would want sized.

The big worry: the debt-funded buildout, both ways

What would ease it: the guarantees never get called (none have been so far); hyperscalers, who pay their own way, keep growing faster than the financed clouds (they did this quarter, $60.1B vs $47.5B); the Fed stops hiking, which lowers the cost of every lease Nvidia is backing; receivables come back toward 60 days. What would make it worse: the Fed hikes on Dec 9 (markets price 91–97%) and keeps going, as St. Louis Fed president Musalem suggested tonight; a large AI lab misses a lease payment; states slow data-center permits, so sites sit idle while leases run; memory prices keep rising faster than Nvidia can pass them on. How Nvidia is placed: $43.8B of free cash flow in one quarter and $53.5B of cash cover a lot. Total guarantees are about 2.7 quarters of free cash flow. The risk isn't solvency; it's that a slice of reported demand is demand Nvidia helped pay for. If you want one number to track each quarter, track that $118.0B.

5. The CEO question

  • Who ran it: Kress, in both jobs. No separate CFO was named. Chair Harvey Jones read the leadership statement.
  • Huang: Jones said he "voluntarily resigned" and that "the board has no intention of bringing him back". He is off the board, with no advisory role mentioned. Huang has said publicly he would "be honored" to return. He testifies before Congress on Dec 1.
  • The search: Heidrick & Struggles, internal and external candidates, no timeline. Jones: "we don't want to hurry".
  • The investigation: an independent board committee is still working; CNBC reported it covers sales to Megaspeed. Reports say the SEC is looking at the departure too.
  • How it sounded: controlled and dull, which is the point. Morningstar's Brian Colello: "Seems like business as usual". Kress closed with "We've got this." Reuters' read, which I share: demand is not the near-term risk any more; execution and governance are.
  • How much rests on one person now: more than before. Kress is carrying the CEO and CFO jobs through a Rubin ramp, a congressional hearing and an open investigation. Until a permanent CEO is named, every rally carries a governance discount.

8. The IV crush lesson, with real numbers

IV (implied volatility) is the price of uncertainty inside an option. Before a report it's high because nobody knows the number. The moment the number is out, that uncertainty is gone and the option loses value even if the stock moves your way. That's IV crush.

The straddle buyer was right that NVDA would move, and still lost 41% at the after-hours price.

9. Bull case vs bear case from here

Bull: the numbers were the best in the group's history: a $7B beat, a guide above the Street, a raised fiscal 2028, $43.8B of free cash flow and $227B of buyback firepower. On your chart, the 0.5 has held two 2-day closes after two wicks under it, RSI is 22.5 (deeply stretched), and the after-hours high cleared the last 2-day high. At about 19 times trailing earnings, it's the cheapest it has been in this cycle. Bear: on your map, the 2.0, 1.618, 1.382 and 0.786 all failed in order since October, and the 2-day highs have fallen eight times in a row. The guide only met the whisper, and four of the last five beats fell the next day. The pop faded from +4.8% to +1.0% before midnight. The CEO seat is empty, the investigation is open, and the balance sheet is now underwriting its customers while the Fed is pricing hikes. My position: the bear case wins on the trend, the bull case wins the next few days. I think the earnings take away the "is demand cracking" fear, so the $155 area holds near term, but the stock doesn't get its multiple back while the CEO chair is empty. I expect a bounce that stalls at $170. I'm about 60/40 on that. What changes my mind: a 2-day close over $170.22 (your 0.786, the double-bottom neckline and the straddle top) opens $182.67 (the 14-day) and then the $201–204 band, and I'd drop the stall view. A close under $155.33 tells me the market doesn't care about the numbers, and $140.93 is next.
