MSFT earnings — Microsoft, fiscal Q1 FY2027

October 28, 202613 min readOpen in the reader

Management sees more cloud demand than it can serve and growth speeding up, and the thing that could spoil it is capacity that arrives late and costs more, which is already pulling the cloud margin down.

Reported 2026-10-28. Next report: not found (Webull lists no date yet; last year's pattern points to late January). Updated 2026-11-12. Research, not investment advice.

The short version

  • Demand: stronger. Azure grew 48% and is guided to 49–50% next quarter; the limit is supply, not orders.
  • Guidance: held. Next-quarter revenue $96.0–97.1B (+18–19%). Calendar-2026 capital spending trimmed to about $170B from about $175B, put down to third-party capacity arriving later.
  • Biggest worry: capacity late, cloud margin slipping (66% now, about 65% guided), with a backlog that stopped growing in the quarter.
  • Chart: leans up, but only just. The 50-day at $518.20 decides it; price closed $521.12.

The numbers

  • Revenue $92.28B (+19%) against a $90.74B estimate, and above the top of July's $89.85–90.95B guide.
  • GAAP EPS $4.92 against a $4.72 estimate (Webull, GAAP to GAAP). The non-GAAP figure is $4.89 because it strips out OpenAI investment effects, so here GAAP is the higher number.
  • Azure revenue $33.2B, about 3 points above the July guide. Operating cash flow $58.0B; free cash flow $18.6B.

What management said about the road ahead

  • Next quarter: revenue $96.0–97.1B; Azure +49–50% in constant currency.
  • Demand vs supply: "Customer demand continues to exceed available supply" (Amy Hood, CFO). Azure growth may swing quarter to quarter with capacity and GPU delivery timing.
  • Backlog: commercial backlog $677B, up 73% from a year ago but flat against $678B in June. About 30% turns into revenue in the next 12 months.
  • Margins: Microsoft Cloud gross margin 66%, guided to about 65%. Depreciation was $11.1B against $7.1B a year ago.
  • Pricing: Satya Nadella described a shift from per-seat pricing toward consumption pricing. Copilot daily users rose almost 50% from the prior quarter.
  • Costs: headcount is down 2% from a year ago and a voluntary exit offer is in next quarter's cost guide.
  • Full year: whether July's FY27 framework was reaffirmed: not found. Segment names changed, so segment guides do not compare with July.

What management is worried about

  1. Capacity arriving late. Last quarter's capital spending ($39.4B cash; about $45.2B with finance leases) came in under its own "over $50B" guide because third-party capacity slipped.
  2. Cloud margin. 66% falling to about 65% as depreciation rises 56% from a year ago.
  3. Over-building. The filing warns capacity could be underused or written down if demand is overestimated; $364.6B of leases have not yet started.
  4. AI partners. Dependence on partners that are also customers and competitors, and the risk of an OpenAI impairment or renegotiation. OpenAI's share of today's backlog: not found.
  5. Power, land, permits and chips. Named as shortages in the filing.
  6. Tax. An IRS claim for an additional $28.9B, which Microsoft is contesting.

The big worry: capacity late and margin slipping, both ways

What would ease it

  • Third-party data centers and GPUs landing on schedule, so next quarter's "over $50B" of spending actually happens and Azure meets 49–50%.
  • Memory prices cooling. Micron says price increases should be more moderate after its current quarter.
  • The backlog growing again from $677B, which would show new orders are replacing what is being delivered.
  • More of the revenue coming from consumption pricing on capacity already built, which fills the buildings faster.

What would make it worse

  • More delivery slips. Oracle says the bottleneck has moved from chips to power to buildings, and its own New Mexico and Wisconsin sites face permit and grid delays.
  • Memory getting dearer. TrendForce (Sept 30) forecast fourth-quarter DRAM contract prices up 10–15% and NAND up 15–20% on the quarter; Micron expects supply to be tighter in 2027 and 2028 than in 2026.
  • OpenAI leaning less on Azure. The April 27, 2026 amendment ended Microsoft's exclusivity and lets OpenAI use any cloud (Directions on Microsoft). The same page put 45% of the backlog two quarters ago down to OpenAI.
  • A flat backlog for a second quarter while the margin keeps falling.

How this company is placed against it

  • Best funded of the group: it paid for the build from cash flow, issued no debt and repaid $4.85B. Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Oracle are all borrowing or selling shares.
  • It still expects positive free cash flow in FY27, so a rate hike in December hurts it less than the debt-funded builders.
  • The OpenAI tie cuts both ways: Microsoft keeps its licence through 2032 and a capped revenue share through 2030 (reported cap $38B, The Information via Reuters), but it no longer has the customer to itself.

Money plans

  • Capital spending: over $50B next quarter; about $170B for calendar 2026. FY27 spending is expected to speed up in the second half; no figure.
  • Buybacks and dividend: buybacks $2.9B (down from $4.0B); dividend $0.98 a share.
  • Debt: none issued. Finance lease liabilities $71.0B; leases not yet started $364.6B.

People and governance

  • CEO: Satya Nadella, CEO since 2014, also chairman since 2021. Not a founder.
  • CFO: Amy Hood, since 2013.
  • Successor named: no. The proxy says the board reviews the succession plan every year and keeps an emergency plan.
  • Control: one share class, one vote a share. No controlling holder found. Chair and CEO are the same person; the lead independent director is Sandra Peterson.
  • Recent changes: a run of senior exits in 2026. Rajesh Jha (retiring, announced March) and Phil Spencer (Xbox, retired February) per GeekWire; communications chief Frank Shaw leaving at year end per Fortune. Reid Hoffman and Hugh Johnston are not standing for the board again at the Dec 8 annual meeting. Investigations: not found.
  • How much rests on one person: a moderate amount. Nadella holds both top jobs and has run the company for 12 years, the CFO for 13, and no successor is named, but there is no founder control and the bench is deep.
