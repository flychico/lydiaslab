Management sees 2027 booked ahead at higher prices, and the one thing that could spoil it is fuel on the part of the bill that is not hedged, landing in an already soft first quarter.

Reported 2026-10-27. Next report: not found. Updated 2026-11-12. Research, not investment advice.

The short version

  • Demand: Stronger. Bookings up in the mid-teens since the last call; 2027 sailings up in the high teens.
  • Guidance: Raised. Full-year adjusted EPS $17.85 to $17.95, from $17.73 to $17.87.
  • Biggest worry: Fuel on the unhedged 42% of 2026 and 47% of 2027, with the Q1 2027 yield headwind a close second.
  • Chart: Leans up. A daily close under $296.44 would end that.

The numbers

  • Revenue $5,591M, up 9%. Roughly in line; sources disagree on a hair above or below (Webull's estimate was $5.61B).
  • GAAP EPS $6.45, adjusted EPS $6.57. The beat is adjusted against adjusted: $6.57 against consensus of $6.38 and the company's own $6.26 to $6.36. The GAAP figure also clears consensus, so no mixing problem here.

What management said about the road ahead

  • Next quarter: Q4 adjusted EPS $3.48 to $3.58, net yields up 4.2% to 4.7% in constant currency, fuel $379M.
  • Full year: Net yields up about 2.1% (prior range 1.75% to 2.25%), costs excluding fuel about flat, fuel expense $1,345M.
  • Bookings: Close-in demand in Europe and the Caribbean and onboard spending drove the Q3 beat.
  • Pricing: No formal 2027 guide. The 2027 EPS target starting with a 20 was reaffirmed.
  • Booking window: The CEO rejected the view that shorter booking windows mean a weaker consumer. He pointed to shorter trips and younger guests.
  • Supply: 2027 capacity up about 4.5%. Yield growth is weighted to the second half, with about 2 points of headwind in Q1 2027.
  • Quote: "2027 bookings are pacing ahead of historical levels at higher rates." (Jason Liberty, Chairman and CEO, press release).

  2. Q1 2027 yield headwind: about 2 points.
  3. Sandals closing timing: 50% of Sandals and Beaches, about $3B per the call recap, closing expected early 2027.

  5. 10-Q risk text: not found. An AI-written summary mentions the Havana Docks litigation; unverified.

The big worry: fuel, both ways

What would ease it

  • Calm in the Strait of Hormuz. Waller at the Fed says insurance and transfers there add $20 to $30 a barrel. Brent was about $92.5 on Nov 11, down from above $110 in late October.
  • Talks holding. Brent fell to $96.54 on Nov 9 on de-escalation hopes, and VLSFO supply improved in Fujairah (ENGINE).
  • Refineries returning. Chevron says about 10% of global refining is offline and expects margins to ease after the near term.
  • The hedge book itself: more than half of 2026 and 2027 is already fixed.

What would make it worse

  • More incidents in the Strait. A tanker hit a mine there on Nov 11 and WTI closed near $87.
  • A slow fix. Exxon's CEO expects product-market normalisation to run well through next year.
  • Distillate staying scarce. On Sept 30 the 20-port average was $860.50 a ton for VLSFO and $1,522 for marine gasoil, and China suspended most October fuel exports (ShipUniverse, Oct 1).
  • Timing. Higher fuel in Q1 2027 would land on the quarter that already carries the 2-point yield headwind.

How Royal Caribbean is placed against it

  • Best placed of your travel names. Hedged 58% for 2026 and 53% for 2027; Carnival hedges nothing, and the US airlines were reported as largely unhedged.
  • It raised full-year guidance while paying more for fuel. Costs excluding fuel are about flat.
  • Hedges roll off: only 29% for 2028. A long shock still reaches it, later.

Money plans

  • Capital spending about $4.6B for 2026.
  • Buying 50% of Sandals and Beaches Resorts (18 resorts, three in development), about $3B per the call recap, closing expected early 2027.
  • Nine-month buybacks $1,035M and dividends $1,075M. No forward figure.
  • Issued $1.25B of notes due 2034 to repay term loans. Liquidity $7.4B. A December Fed hike is priced, which matters for any new borrowing.

  • Counterweight: John Brock is lead independent director. Fain stays on the board.
