NOW earnings — ServiceNow, Q3 2026

October 28, 202612 min readOpen in the reader

Management sees AI adding to what customers pay and says the plan is working; the thing that could spoil it is a forward backlog that is growing more slowly, which is where a loss of seats to AI would show up first.

Reported 2026-10-28. Next report: not found. Updated 2026-11-12. Research, not investment advice.

The short version

  • Demand: steady. Revenue grew about 20%; AI net new contract value grew 65%.
  • Guidance: raised slightly. 2026 subscription revenue $15.780–15.790B (from $15.760–15.780B); free cash flow margin 36% (from 35%); operating margin 31.5% and subscription gross margin 81% held.
  • Biggest worry: forward backlog. Q4 growth in current backlog (cRPO) is guided to 18.5%, down from about 21% in Q3.
  • Chart: leans down. Price is under all three averages; a close back above the 200-day ($113.96) would flip it.

The numbers

  • Revenue $4.11B (+20.5%) vs $4.09B estimate, a slight beat.
  • Non-GAAP EPS $1.00 vs $1.02 consensus (Constellation Research), a slight miss. GAAP EPS $0.27 vs $0.33 on Webull. Those are two separate like-for-like comparisons; do not mix them.
  • Share prices and EPS reflect the 5-for-1 split of Dec 17, 2025.

What management said about the road ahead

  • Next quarter: subscription revenue $4.245–4.255B; cRPO growth 18.5%.
  • Backlog: the slowdown was put down to renewals pulled forward into Q3, more self-hosted public-sector deals that do not count in backlog, and service bundling. The CFO called cRPO an imperfect predictor.
  • AI revenue: 2026 AI target raised to at least $1.7B (from $1.5B in May and $1B at the start).
  • Pricing: customers want seat subscriptions plus usage-based pricing, not outcome-based pricing. Consumption is still early. "So the strategy is working, and yet we're still in the early days." (Bill McDermott, CEO; third-party transcript)
  • Partners: AI labs are presented as partners: an expanded OpenAI tie-up and an alliance including Anthropic, AWS and Nvidia.
  • 2027: no numbers. One recap (nowben) has McDermott placing real acceleration in 2027.
  • Not addressed: whether AI reduces the number of seats customers buy.

What management is worried about

  1. Backlog growth slowing. cRPO growth from about 21% to a guided 18.5%.
  2. Consumption pricing is early. The revenue model is shifting before the new stream is large: the AI target is $1.7B against $15.8B of subscription revenue.
  3. Debt from acquisitions. Long-term debt $5.44B (from $1.49B) plus $2.09B of commercial paper, after three deals worth about $11.2B together (my sum).
  4. A Department of Justice investigation tied to a government contract, still open in the Q3 10-Q.
  5. Filing risk factors: the 10-Q states no material changes since the annual report.

The big worry: slowing backlog and the seat question, both ways

What would ease it

  • cRPO growth coming in above 18.5% in Q4, which would support the timing explanation.
  • AI contract value still compounding: +65% this quarter and a target raised twice this year.
  • Usage revenue becoming large enough to see. Microsoft's CEO described the same move from per-seat to consumption pricing, so customers are being taught this model by more than one vendor.
  • AI labs staying partners. Palantir's management calls the model makers market-expanders for software that sits on top of them.

What would make it worse

  • A second quarter of slower backlog growth, which would look like a trend and not timing.
  • Customers hiring fewer people. The Fed's Beige Book had five districts reporting firms trimming staff or delaying hiring after investing in AI; fewer employees means fewer seats.
  • Gross margin slipping as AI usage costs rise. One recap (nowben) puts gross profit at about 80 cents per subscription dollar against 83 before.
  • Peers losing ground. Palantir's management was openly critical of software firms that resell AI usage as revenue, and frames this as winners and losers.

How this company is placed against it

  • Growth is steady near 20%, not accelerating; Palantir is growing 96%. The market is paying for proof, and the proof here is slow.
  • Cash is strong: free cash flow margin 36%, capex cut to $91M in the quarter, and management says it is not short of computing capacity.
  • Less cushion than a year ago: debt rose to fund Moveworks (about $2.4B), Veza (about $1.2B) and Armis (about $7.6B), all closed.

Money plans

  • Capex $91M in the quarter vs $235M a year ago; being reduced.
  • No buybacks in Q3; $2.225B in nine months; about $4.2B of authorisation left.
  • Long-term debt $5.44B plus $2.09B commercial paper. No dividend found.
  • Headcount slightly down: more quota-carrying sales staff and AI engineers, fewer support roles.

People and governance

  • CEO: Bill McDermott, since November 2019, not a founder. He is also chairman (since October 2022), so the roles are not split. Lead independent director: Susan Bostrom.
  • CFO: Gina Mastantuono, titled President and CFO in the Q3 release. Start date: not found. The April proxy lists a different officer as President and Chief Legal Officer; the two titles could not be reconciled.
  • Successor named: no.
  • Control: single share class; no controlling holder found.
  • Recent changes or investigations: the 10-Q says the Department of Justice has opened its own investigation into the company's 2023 hiring of the Army's former chief information officer; timing and outcome unknown. In February 2026 McDermott agreed to buy $3M of stock and five executives cancelled selling plans, as reported by Panabee (a page that says it is AI-generated). Departures: not found.
  • How much rests on one person: a fair amount. McDermott is chairman, CEO and the voice of the AI story, with no successor named; the open federal investigation is the item to keep an eye on.
