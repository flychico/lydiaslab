Beige Book — November 26, 2025

November 26, 20252 min readOpen in the reader

Official report: Summary · Full PDF Local PDF: 2025-11-26 Beige Book.pdf

Overall economic activity

Activity was "little changed" in most Districts; two noted a modest decline and one reported modest growth. Manufacturing rose somewhat in most Districts, though tariffs and tariff uncertainty remained headwinds. Residential construction fell in some Districts; a few reported continued office real estate recovery. Energy and agriculture were largely stable (low oil and some crop prices noted). Loan demand was mixed, and nonfinancial services revenues were mostly flat to down. Outlooks were largely unchanged, with some contacts seeing higher risk of slower activity and some manufacturers optimistic. The report was prepared by the Dallas Fed.

Consumer spending

Overall spending fell further, but higher-end retail stayed resilient. Some retailers said the government shutdown hurt purchases, and auto dealers reported lower EV sales after the federal tax credit expired. Travel and tourism were roughly unchanged.

Labor markets

Employment declined slightly, and about half of Districts reported weaker labor demand. Layoff announcements rose, but more Districts reported hiring freezes, replacement-only hiring and attrition than layoffs; some employers cut hours instead. Workers were easier to find in most Districts, with pockets of difficulty for skilled roles and due to fewer immigrant workers. Wage growth was modest, more moderate in manufacturing, construction and health care. Rising health insurance premiums kept pushing up labor costs.

Prices

Prices rose moderately. Input cost pressure was widespread in manufacturing and retail, largely from tariffs, plus insurance, utilities, technology and health care. Pass-through varied; Kansas City said most firms passed through only about 20 percent of higher input costs. Several reports described margin compression. Some material prices fell (weak demand, deferred or lower tariffs). Contacts expected cost pressure to persist; near-term price-hike plans were mixed.

Notable themes

  • Government shutdown: Hurt retail sales and disrupted SNAP benefits, raising food-assistance demand.
  • AI: A few firms said AI replaced entry-level positions or slowed hiring through productivity gains.
  • Tariffs: Driving input costs, margin pressure and uncertainty.
  • EV tax credit expiration: Lower EV sales.

District highlights

  • Boston: Slight expansion on stronger home sales; cautiously optimistic.
  • New York: Modest decline; layoffs at some major employers.
  • Philadelphia: Modest decline worsened by the shutdown; shifting policies stressed small businesses.
  • Cleveland: Some manufacturers benefited from AI data center demand.
  • Chicago: Slight gains across most sectors.
  • St. Louis: Shutdown amplified weaker demand; outlook pessimistic.
  • Kansas City: Growth slowed slightly; firms optimistic about hiring over six months.
  • Dallas: Activity weakened slightly; outlooks worsened.

Read-through for SPY

  • A stalling economy with a softening labor market strengthens the case for Fed easing, typically supportive of equities.
  • Weak mass-market consumer spending versus resilient high-end demand favors selectivity within consumer stocks.
  • Limited tariff pass-through and margin compression are a headwind for corporate earnings.
