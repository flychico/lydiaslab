JPM earnings — JPMorgan Chase, Q3 2026

October 13, 202614 min readOpen in the reader

Management sees a resilient consumer and more interest income as rates rise, and the thing that could spoil it is the same rise in rates going far enough to turn a credit market where lending terms have loosened.

Reported Oct 13, 2026. Next report: Jan 14, 2027. Updated Nov 12, 2026. Research, not investment advice.

The short version

  • Demand: steady. Card spending +10%; investment banking fees +14%; markets revenue +15%.
  • Guidance: raised. 2026 net interest income about $106.2B (was about $105.5B) and adjusted expense about $108.6B (was $107.5B). Card charge-off rate held at about 3.2%.
  • Biggest worry: higher rates meeting loosened lending terms. The CEO told people to be ready for a 6% 10-year yield; it is at about 5% now.
  • Chart: no lean. Price is between $337.83 and $346.22; the levels that decide it are $359.80 above and $324.25 below. These are my lines, not yours.

The numbers

  • EPS $5.57, or $5.75 excluding securities losses, against about $5.91 expected. A miss on either basis.
  • Reported revenue $50.2B against $51.2B. Also a miss. Net income $15.3B.
  • Credit costs were $2.7B: $2.2B of charge-offs plus a $539M reserve build, mostly on business lending.

What management said about the road ahead

  • Next quarter: net interest income about $28B; expense about $27.5B.
  • 2027: net interest income excluding Markets of about $107B, which counts on three rate rises in the forward curve. An expense consensus near $114B was called not far off.
  • Consumer: resilient because of the job market. Card charge-off rate 2.91%, down from 3.15% a year ago; late payments fell.
  • Reserves: the CFO tied the build to new lending, not downgrades.
  • Credit terms: the CEO said terms across the market have loosened and the bank is tightening its own.
  • Deals: the pipeline slowed after a late-September rate sell-off.
  • Stance: "we remain prepared for a wide range of outcomes." Jamie Dimon, Chairman and CEO.

What management is worried about

  1. Rates higher for longer. The CEO raised a 6% 10-year yield and more Fed rises, and said it was not a prediction. Deposits can move when rates rise.
  2. Loosened credit terms. Weaker covenants and interest paid in kind were cited; reserves built by $539M.
  3. AI concentration. AI-linked lending and equity exposure, and AI agents, are named risks; no figure given.
  4. Leverage outside banks. Hedge funds and leveraged finance.
  5. The macro list. Large fiscal deficits, sticky inflation, high asset prices and geopolitics.
  6. Cost growth. Expense up 12%, with the 2026 guide raised by about $1.1B.

The big worry: higher rates meeting loose credit, both ways

What would ease it

  • Inflation cooling. The Fed desk doc shows the Fed's own projections with inflation falling toward 2% next year and the policy rate drifting down from 2028. The next CPI is Dec 10.
  • Jobs holding. Payrolls rose 165,000 in October with unemployment at 4.1%, and jobs are what management credits for card credit staying clean.
  • Peers staying quiet on credit. Bank of America's allowance fell about $109M in Q3 and Citigroup's credit losses were flat.
  • Lighter capital rules. The re-proposed capital package would cut required capital for the largest banks by a net 6% (Congressional Research Service); it is not final.

What would make it worse

  • The 10-year pushing from 5% toward 6%. It touched 5% and closed near 4.98% on Nov 10 after core CPI rose 0.3% for a third month; futures put about a 90% chance on a December hike, and two Fed officials already voted for one in October.
  • Losses in private credit. In his April letter the CEO wrote that credit standards had weakened modestly almost everywhere, with private credit at $1.8 trillion (Financial Times, via the Irish Times). In February a UK lender, Market Financial Solutions, collapsed with a reported $1.3B collateral shortfall and bank shares fell as much as 6% in a day.
  • Early signs at other banks. Wells Fargo's provision rose to $1.03B from $681M on commercial and auto loans; Bank of America's commercial property charge-offs jumped to $105M from $2M a quarter earlier, and its exposure to asset managers and funds grew to $168.0B.
  • A deal market that stays shut. Fees were up 14%, but management already said the pipeline slowed.

How this company is placed against it

  • Rising rates help before they hurt: the 2027 interest-income plan is built on three more hikes. The damage comes later, through deposits moving, bond losses and borrowers failing.
  • Capital is deep: a 13.9% core capital ratio in the release against an 11.5% requirement, and about $30B of excess. That is why buybacks were cut to $664M; management would sooner keep the capital to deploy.
  • It has already taken one lesson: a $170M loss on the Tricolor bankruptcy in 2025, and it says it is now tightening while others loosen.

Money plans

  • Net buybacks cut to $664M in the quarter, despite a new $50B authorisation from July 1.
  • Dividend raised to $1.65 a quarter from $1.50.
  • Expense up 12%; headcount 322,964, up about 4,800 in a year.
  • Stress capital buffer 2.5%, unchanged through Sept 30, 2027.

People and governance

  • CEO: Jamie Dimon, 70, CEO since January 2006 and also Chairman. Not a founder.
  • CFO: Jeremy Barnum, since May 2021.
  • Successor named: no single name, but the field narrowed this year. On June 25 Doug Petno (61) and Troy Rohrbaugh (56) were named co-presidents, each with a $30M retention award. Two executives told Reuters that Rohrbaugh leads internally; analyst Mike Mayo gives Petno a slight edge.
  • Timeline, as reported: CNBC's sources say Dimon stays CEO about three more years and then becomes executive chairman. In July he said the transition could be several years away and is the board's call.
  • Control: one class of stock, no controlling holder. Vanguard 9.86%, BlackRock 7.15%.
  • Chair: combined with the CEO role. Board policy is to split them at the next CEO change. Lead independent director: Stephen Burke.
  • Recent changes: Marianne Lake, long seen as a contender, is retiring. Jennifer Piepszak stays as chief operating officer and has said she does not want the top job.
  • Lawsuit naming the CEO: NPR reported that on Jan 22, 2026 President Trump sued the bank and Dimon for at least $5B, alleging accounts were closed in 2021 for political reasons. The bank said the suit has no merit. Current status: not found.
  • How much rests on one person: a great deal. The same man has been CEO for nearly 21 years and holds both top jobs. The offset is that the handover is being planned in the open, with two named front-runners.

Does the chart agree?

  • Momentum: RSI 54.0, neutral.
  • Ceiling: $346.22 first (38.2% of the Oct 1 low $324.25 to the Nov 5 high), then $359.80, then the 52-week high of $364.68 from Aug 13. The highs have been getting lower: $364.68, $361.06, $359.80. On Nov 5 price hit $359.80 and closed at $337.34.
  • Floor: $337.83 (61.8%), then $324.25 (the Oct 1 low) with the 200-day just under it at $321.98.
  • Your tested edge: does not apply. Price and the 50-day are above the 200-day, but RSI is 54, not under 40.
  • Scorecard: no open or watching play on JPM.
  • Verdict: the chart supports neither direction. That matches a quarter that missed while the outlook went up, and a CEO who is cautious while earning more. A close above $359.80 would flip it up; a close below $324.25 would flip it down. The lines are mine; you have not drawn JPM.

What would change this read

  • The Fed on Dec 9 and CPI on Dec 10: a half-point hike or a 10-year yield well above 5% leans toward the worry.
  • The Q4 report on Jan 14, 2027: the reserve build, the card charge-off rate against 3.2%, and the first full 2027 guide.
  • A close above $359.80 or below $324.25.
