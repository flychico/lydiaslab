RTX earnings — RTX Corporation, Q3 2026

October 20, 202613 min readOpen in the reader

Management sees sales, profit and cash growing into 2027 on a record order book, and the thing that could spoil it is suppliers: castings, rocket motors and electronic parts decide how fast those orders turn into deliveries.

Reported 2026-10-20. Next report: not found (the Q4 call and detailed 2027 outlook are promised for January). Updated 2026-11-12. Research, not investment advice.

The short version

  • Demand: Stronger. Record backlog of $340B, with more than $81B of awards in the quarter.
  • Guidance: Raised. Adjusted sales $97.0B to $97.5B (was $95.0B to $96.0B); adjusted EPS $7.65 to $7.75 (was $7.10 to $7.25); free cash flow $9.0B to $9.2B (was $8.50B to $8.75B). A little under half of the EPS raise is a one-time tariff recovery.
  • Biggest worry: Supply limits on the munitions ramp, with the continuing resolution second.
  • Chart: No lean. It sits between $197.12 and $202.72 and has failed to hold above $202.72.

The numbers

  • Sales $24,965M, up 11%, against about $24.0B to $24.1B expected. Beat.
  • Be careful with the EPS beat. Adjusted EPS was $2.26 against $1.78 expected, but $0.25 of that is a one-time recovery of tariff costs. Without it, about $2.01. GAAP EPS was $1.89. The $1.78 estimate is compared with the adjusted figure, not GAAP.
  • Free cash flow $2.1B. About $0.3B of operating cash flow came from the tariff recovery.

What management said about the road ahead

  • 2027: Growth in sales, profit and cash. No figures until January.
  • Orders: Defense orders about 4.6 times sales, led by Tomahawk and AMRAAM. Backlog is $176B commercial and $164B defense; international is 37%.
  • Munitions output: Up over 90% this year, with plans to raise rates two to four times over seven to ten years (call).
  • Supply: Chris Calio, Chairman and CEO, called supply chain the "single biggest enabling factor" for higher rates (call). He said RTX is competing with the AI buildout for some inputs.
  • Engines: Geared turbofan groundings were stable in Q3, with reductions expected in Q4. Compensation payments are tailing off.
  • Aftermarket: Commercial aftermarket up 16% organically.
  • Budget: Under a continuing resolution; management expects munitions to be prioritized.

What management is worried about

  1. Castings and rocket motors. Named as constraints; the rocket motor supply base is concentrated.
  2. Electronic parts. Shortages of circuit boards and components.
  3. Continuing resolution. Funding at last year's levels through Dec 11.
  4. Engine upgrade execution at Pratt & Whitney.
  5. Customer credit. A $363M charge tied to a customer bankruptcy (customer not named).
  6. The tariff benefit will not repeat. $0.25 of Q3 adjusted EPS.

The big worry: suppliers on the munitions ramp, both ways

What would ease it

  • New rocket motor capacity. The Pentagon is putting $1B into L3Harris's missile motor business (Breaking Defense, Jan 13). RTX says it is adding partners among other motor suppliers.
  • RTX's own casting investment in Asheville, and government help securing priority supply and critical minerals (call).
  • Firm multi-year orders that let suppliers invest. The Navy's seven-year, $22.9B Tomahawk award (The National, Aug 17) firms up one of five February framework agreements.
  • Full-year funding. The House budget plan carried $21B to speed munitions buying and expand lines (CSIS).

What would make it worse

  • Only two established US solid rocket motor makers remain, down from six in 2000; newer entrants are mostly at prototype or low-rate output (SpaceNews, June 14, via a news summary page).
  • Motor output is hard to rush: curing and inspection set the pace (Breaking Defense, July 30).
  • The targets are steep. Tomahawk is meant to go from 60 a year to more than 1,000 (The National).
  • Another stopgap or a shutdown after Dec 11. A Gray DC report (Nov 7) says the majority in both chambers flips and a shutdown before the holidays is possible.
  • AI data center demand keeps taking the same electronic parts.

How this company is placed against it

  • More than half the backlog is commercial, so the company is not riding on munitions alone.
  • No immediate plan to buy a motor supplier (call), so RTX depends on others' capacity.
  • Lockheed and Boeing say the same thing: production, not orders, is the limit. Lockheed has signed large PAC-3 propulsion contracts, so the primes are competing for the same motors.

Money plans

  • Q3 capex $749M and company R&D $711M. Capacity additions in Mississippi, Poland and Asheville (call).
  • No buybacks in nine months ($50M a year earlier). Dividends paid $2,882M in nine months; any increase: not found.
  • A January 7 executive order bars buybacks and dividends at contractors the Pentagon judges to be underperforming. Reuters (Feb 6) reported the President singled out the Raytheon unit as the least responsive to the Pentagon. The Pentagon's list was not public as of Feb 9 (Breaking Defense); whether RTX is on it: not found. Calio said on Jan 27 that RTX remained committed to the dividend (Reuters via BNN Bloomberg).
  • $526M of debt repaid in nine months. The 10-year is near 5% (Fed Desk).

People and governance

  • CEO: Chris Calio, CEO since May 2024, chairman since April 30, 2025. Not a founder; he was president and operating chief and succeeded Greg Hayes (GovCon Wire; Defense News).
  • CFO: Neil Mitchill (Q3 call). Start date: not found.
  • Successor named: Not found.
  • Chair/CEO: Combined. Lead independent director: not found. All ten directors were re-elected and pay approved on April 30, 2026; no shareholder proposals were on the ballot (8-K summary).
  • Control: Dual-class shares or a controlling holder: not found. The one holder list opened showed nobody above 2.2%, but it looked incomplete.
  • Recent changes or investigations: Hayes's adviser role was due to end Jan 2, 2026 (not confirmed that it did). Pratt & Whitney president Shane Eddy is retiring; Jill Albertelli takes over Jan 1, 2027 (RTX, Sept 15). New investigations in the last 12 months: not found.
  • How much rests on one person: Moderate. Chair and CEO are one person and no successor is named, and the unit with the engine problems changes leaders in January.
