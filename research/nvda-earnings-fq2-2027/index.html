NVDA earnings — Nvidia, fiscal Q2 2027

August 26, 202611 min readOpen in the reader

Nvidia's last report said demand outruns supply through fiscal 2028, but that report is 11 weeks old, and since Nov 8 the open question is who will be running the company.

Reported Aug 26, 2026. Next report: Nov 17, 2026. Updated Nov 11, 2026. Research, not investment advice.

The short version

  • Demand: stronger. Supply is the limit.
  • Guidance: raised. Next-quarter revenue $108B, against $91B guided a quarter earlier.
  • Biggest worry in the filing: Nvidia is financing its own customers (up to $108.5B of guarantees).
  • Biggest worry now: leadership. The board is reported to be weighing the CEO's future.
  • Chart: down. Below every average, and under its 52-week low after hours.

The numbers

  • Revenue $96.2B, up 106%, against a $92.2B estimate. Data Center $89.0B.
  • GAAP EPS $2.46 against $2.06 estimated. Gross margin 75.0%.

What management said about the road ahead

  • Next quarter: revenue $108.0B, plus or minus 2%. Gross margin 74.0%, one point lower than before.
  • Further out: gross margin expected to bottom at 71%–72%, then settle at 72%–73% in fiscal 2028. Revenue growth of about 70% in fiscal 2028 was given as an early view.
  • Supply: expected to stay the bottleneck at least through the end of fiscal 2028. In the CFO's words: "This is a supply-constrained outlook." (Colette Kress).
  • New platform: Vera Rubin began shipping in August, expected to be about 20% of Data Center revenue next quarter.
  • China: the outlook assumes no data center revenue from China.

What management is worried about

  1. Memory costs. Rising faster than expected and the main drag on margin.
  2. Its own commitments. Supply and capacity commitments were $279B, up from $119B a quarter earlier.
  3. Guarantees for customers. Up to $108.5B, mostly data center leases for an OpenAI affiliate.
  4. Stretching payment terms. Some large builds got 90 days to a year.
  5. Concentration. One direct customer was 16% of revenue.
  6. China. It describes itself as effectively shut out of the data center market there.

The big worry: leadership

What has been reported (press reports, not company statements)

  • Sunday Nov 8: reports that the CEO, Jensen Huang, admitted to an affair with a woman US intelligence is said to have identified as a Chinese spy, and that the board opened an investigation.
  • Nov 11: the Wall Street Journal, citing unnamed sources, reported the board is preparing to remove him and could meet as soon as that day. The New York Times reported a second affair, with an executive who reports to him.
  • No statement from Nvidia or Mr. Huang was found in the coverage opened. No decision has been confirmed.

Was this a risk you could have seen?

  • The event, no. Nobody models personal conduct.
  • The exposure, yes. Three facts were public before any of this: he has led the company for 33 years as co-founder, he has about 60 direct reports by his own account, and no successor had been named. That is a company where one person holds an unusual share of the decisions.
  • Our research missed it. The earnings write-up covered demand, margins and financing and never asked who runs the company. That is why every earnings page now has a People and governance section.

What the numbers say about leadership versus demand

  • The demand is not unique to Nvidia. AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Dell, Super Micro, TSMC and Bloom all raised guidance and all said demand exceeds supply. Orders in hand do not leave with a CEO.
  • What does rest on leadership: the $279B of supply commitments, the $108.5B of guarantees, and the relationships with a handful of very large customers.
  • The price timeline: record high $243.37 on Oct 6. Close $186.64 on Nov 6, before the first report. Close $173.73 on Nov 11, then $158.76 after hours. Most of the fall from the high came before the first report.

What would settle it, either way

  • Calmer: a board statement, a named successor or interim, the investigation closing, and Nov 17 guidance held.
  • Worse: a contested exit, a national-security review touching export licences, customers or suppliers reworking commitments, or the Nov 17 report being delayed.

Money plans

  • Buybacks $19.7B and dividends $6.0B in the quarter. About $99B of buyback authorisation left.
  • $25B of notes issued in June. About $99B held in equity investments.

People and governance

  • CEO: Jensen Huang, co-founder, 33 years. Future under board review, per press reports.
  • CFO: Colette Kress.
  • Successor named: none found.
  • Concentration: about 60 direct reports to the CEO.
  • Board make-up, insider ownership: not yet researched.

Does the chart agree?

  • Trend: down. Close $173.73 is below the 20-day ($203.80), 200-day ($204.19) and 50-day ($217.10).
  • Momentum: RSI 28.7, oversold.
  • Floor: the 52-week low is $163.90 (Mar 30). After hours on Nov 11 it traded $158.76, under that low, with a low of $156.20.
  • Your tested edge: "uptrend plus RSI under 40" does not apply, because price is under the 200-day. An oversold reading in a downtrend has not been an edge in your tests.
  • Verdict: the chart supports the downside and gives no entry either way before Nov 17. Earnings fall inside every near-dated contract, which has made the options expensive.

What would change this read

  • The board's decision.
  • Nov 17: whether the $108B guide and the 71%–72% margin floor hold, and whether guarantees and commitments grew again.
  • A close back above the 200-day, about $204.

Confidence and sources

High on results and guidance (company release, company transcript, 10-Q). The leadership section rests on one article read in full (TechCrunch, citing the Wall Street Journal and New York Times); CNBC, Axios and The Hill would not open. Treat it as reported, not confirmed.
