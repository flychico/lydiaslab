Edge Study — Do our setups beat random?

October 17, 20264 min readOpen in the reader

Short answer: so far the edge leans upside, and it's modest. Buying pullbacks inside uptrends did a bit better than buying on any random day. Betting on the downside with puts did worse than a coin flip. And Fibonacci pullbacks alone added almost nothing beyond simply being long in a rising market.

How it was tested

  • Symbols: the watchlist (SPY, AAPL, TSLA, XOM) plus 18 others (NVDA, MU, AMD, MSFT, META, AMZN, GOOGL, CVX, LMT, PLTR, JPM, QQQ, IWM, XLE, TLT, GLD, USO, SMH).
  • Holding period: 20 trading days (about one month, like a 30–45 day option).
  • No double counting: after a signal, the same symbol must wait 10 days before it can signal again.
  • "Option beats premium": the share of times the 20-day move was bigger than a rough at-the-money option cost (about 0.4 × recent volatility × √20). It's a rough proxy, and real option prices are usually higher, so treat it as a best case.

What this means

  1. Upside edge, modest: "uptrend + RSI under 40" beat random by about 0.5 points of average return and 4 points of win rate (all symbols). That's real but small. It's a Medallion-style edge: small, and only useful repeated with discipline.
  2. No downside edge yet: put setups lost in a period when markets mostly rose (2023–2026). That says more about the bull market than about puts in general.
  3. Fibonacci alone isn't an edge here. It adds value as a map for entries, stops and targets, and when it lines up with RSI or moving averages, but not as a standalone signal. AAPL was the exception.
  4. Options are hard mode: even the best setup beat the option premium only about half the time. Winners must be allowed to run and losers cut early for the math to work.
  5. TSLA behaves differently. Treat it as its own case. Its own downtrend setups did better than the bullish ones.

Caveats

  • Only about four years of data, mostly a bull market, so short setups are likely understated.
  • Sample sizes per ticker are small (6–20 events).
  • No commissions, slippage or real option prices.
  • Next step: add the 2022 bear market, test Fib + RSI together, and track real or paper results in the Trade Log.
