What's going on

1. Tariffs: a headline, not a list, yet

Evidence: Reuters (4:32 a.m.): the administration "could unveil new duties on a broad range of imports from 16 major US trading partners following a probe into excess industrial capacity." Marketspeaker: China and the EU among them; final rates and dates not announced. No official quoted. The Nasdaq is down twice as much as the Dow; Korea fell 6.2%. What it means: markets hate a tariff they can't price; chips and AI hardware with long overseas supply chains sell first, defensive domestic names (KO +0.9%) hold. What would prove me wrong: a narrow list or a long delay before the close, with SMH back over $426.75.

2. The AI hardware unwind: Dell's beat couldn't hold

Evidence: Dell raised its year to $202B and reported a $132.3B AI backlog, traded about $307.7 after hours, and is $276.19 pre-market (−6.5%). Reuters: Micron, AMD and Marvell down more than 5%, Nvidia −4.9%. CNBC (via Marketspeaker): heavy AI bond issuance and weaker free cash flow are making lenders more selective. What it means: Dell beat, raised its year, and is opening 6.5% lower. The market graded the margin footnote, not the headline. DELL is under the 61.8% of its Jan → Sep run ($294.87); the 78.6% ($213.37) is next. What would prove me wrong: DELL back over $294.87 by the close.

3. PCE and GDP at 8:30

  • Numbers vs consensus (expected): core PCE +0.3% m/m, 3.2% y/y (prior 3.1%); headline +0.3%, 3.8% (prior 3.6%); Q3 GDP 3.5% (prior 3.4%); spending +0.4%; claims about 200K; durable goods +0.5%.
  • Guidance and risk: December hike near certain; the number moves January (about 63%). Hot core helps the IWM and BAC put ideas; soft core (+0.1%) could squeeze every put idea at once.
  • Chart and trade verdict: let the first 15-minute candles after 9:30 decide the IWM trigger, not the 8:30 headline.
