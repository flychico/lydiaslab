Weekly Coaching — Week of Nov 2–6, 2026

November 6, 202620 min readOpen in the reader

One bad day broke the floor. Now $745 is the ceiling.

Here’s what I’m seeing. The week started calm, rallied on Wednesday after the midterms, then fell apart Thursday in the worst day since April 2025. SPY finished the week down 2.7%. The travel names held up and the AI names did not. Let’s grade our calls, then set up next week.

The one thing

SPY’s old floor at $744–745 is now its ceiling. The real floor is $722–728, where the 200-day sits. Until SPY closes back above $745, keep trades small and favor the names that held up.

SPY closed Friday at $735.29 (the S&P 500 at 7,376.86). Thursday it fell 4.4% after Democrats won Congress on a platform that includes limits on data centers. Chip earnings from Qualcomm and Arm also disappointed. The chip ETF fell about 11% that day. Friday’s bounce was small: SPY reached $742.21 and stalled just under the old floor. A hot jobs report (165,000 jobs vs about 70,000 expected) raised the odds of a December rate hike to about 77%. A close back above $745 says the scare is over. A close below $722 says the uptrend is broken and cash is a position.

Lesson of the week: Relative strength: who held up when the market fell

Relative strength compares a stock’s move to the market’s move. On a bad day, the stocks that fall the least are the ones big buyers refuse to sell. When the market recovers, those names usually lead.

  • Thursday: SPY: −4.4% (the market)
  • Thursday: HLT: −1.8% (held up)
  • Thursday: NVDA: −9.3% (sold hard)
  • Week: HLT vs SPY: +6.5 pts (HLT +3.8% vs SPY −2.7%)

Here’s the check: divide HLT by SPY. On Oct 30 it was 356.92 ÷ 755.72 = 0.472. Friday it was 370.45 ÷ 735.29 = 0.504. A rising ratio means HLT is beating the market. That’s why our long ideas are HLT and DAL, not NVDA. In a weak tape, buy the leaders, not the losers.

Fib lesson of the week — Fibonacci Playbook (concept 6): Role reversal: HLT’s old ceiling became its floor

When price breaks above an old high, that high often becomes support on the way back down. Sellers who waited there are gone, and buyers who missed the breakout step in. It’s strongest when a Fib level sits at the same price.

The swing is the Sep 9 low at $300.00 (where this leg started) to the Nov 5 record high at $381.34. The old ceiling is the Oct 21 high at $364.50, which capped HLT until Monday’s close above it.

  • 23.6% retracement = 381.34 − (381.34 − 300.00) × 0.236 = 381.34 − 19.20 = $362.14
  • Old high = $364.50 → floor zone $362.14–364.50
  • Target: 0.786 extension = 335.23 + (364.50 − 300.00) × 0.786 = $385.93
