LMT earnings — Lockheed Martin, Q3 2026

October 22, 202613 min readOpen in the reader

Management sees orders and cash rising into 2027 on missile demand, and the thing that could spoil it is Washington paying late: F-35 Lot 20 costs are already running past the money authorized, under a stopgap budget.

Reported 2026-10-22. Next report: not found (2027 guidance is promised for January). Updated 2026-11-12. Research, not investment advice.

The short version

  • Demand: Stronger. Record backlog of $292.0B, up 27% in one quarter.
  • Guidance: Raised, second quarter running. Sales $80.0B to $82.0B (was $79.75B to $81.75B); EPS $30.35 to $30.75 (was $29.95 to $30.65); free cash flow $7.8B to $8.0B (was $7.0B to $7.2B).
  • Biggest worry: F-35 Lot 20 has no final contract, and the government is on a continuing resolution through Dec 11.
  • Chart: Leans up while it closes above $595.88. A close under $558.12 ends that.

The numbers

  • Sales $20,711M, up 11%, against about $20.5B expected. EPS $7.97 against about $7.52 expected. Both beat. The consensus comes from Webull and one secondary source.
  • Free cash flow $3.0B in the quarter.
  • F-35 deliveries: 43 in the quarter, 94 in nine months against 143 a year earlier.

What management said about the road ahead

  • 2027: Growth in all four segments and high single-digit company revenue growth. Formal figures come in January.
  • Backlog: $292.0B, including $52.5B from the multi-year PAC-3 interceptor award.
  • Missiles: Missiles and Fire Control grows about 20% this year; mid-teens expected over five years (call).
  • F-35: Lot awards slipped into 2027, moving a sales bump from 2026 to 2027. Lot 20 costs began to exceed authorized funding in Q3.
  • Budget: Jim Taiclet, Chairman, President and CEO, said continuing resolutions affect timing and new starts but "they don't change the direction of demand" (call).
  • Supply: Large supplier contracts signed for PAC-3 propulsion and seekers; about 750 munitions suppliers to manage.

What management is worried about

  1. F-35 Lot 20 funding gap. Costs on the advance contract now exceed what the government has authorized and funded. The release says results and cash flow suffer if that is not fixed. No dollar figure was given.
  2. Continuing resolutions and shutdowns. The fiscal year opened on a stopgap.
  3. Munitions contract terms. Profit and schedule terms on the long-term agreements are not finished.
  4. Supplier bottlenecks. About 750 munitions suppliers.
  5. Workforce ramp. More than 3,000 hires this year.
  6. Pension. A contribution above $1B is required in 2027.

The big worry: Washington's money for the F-35, both ways

What would ease it

  • Congress passes full-year defense funding before the stopgap ends Dec 11. CSIS notes full-year defense bills passed in lame-duck sessions for fiscal 2015 and 2023.
  • A signed Lot 20 contract, or more interim funding on the advance contract.
  • The defense policy bill becomes law. Both House and Senate versions carry multi-year F-35 buying authority (AeroTime, Oct 22), which the 10-year framework for at least 850 jets (Lots 21 to 30) needs.

What would make it worse

  • Another stopgap into 2027. A Gray DC report (Nov 7) says the majority in both chambers flips after the midterms and a shutdown before the holidays is possible. CSIS (Oct 28) says a new stopgap is likely if Democrats won a chamber; an analyst quoted by Air & Space Forces Magazine thinks the base budget may not clear until spring 2027.
  • The House defense bill has not passed the full House; the Senate committee has not moved its bill (CSIS).
  • Lot 20 talks dragging further while Lockheed keeps building on its own cash.

How this company is placed against it

  • Cash cushion: free cash flow guidance was raised by $0.8B even with this gap open.
  • Early contract actions let munitions production start ahead of appropriations (call).
  • RTX and Boeing describe the same thing: the limit is production and funding timing, not orders.

Money plans

  • Capex about $2.1B to $2.3B this year; $8B of capacity investment through 2030 across more than 20 sites (call). AeroTime separately reports at least $7B self-funded on the F-35 under the framework; whether the two overlap: not found.
  • Quarterly dividend raised $0.15 to $3.60 (call transcript only, not seen in a primary document). Dividends paid $2,408M in nine months.
  • No buybacks in nine months, against $2,250M a year earlier. A January 7 executive order bars buybacks and dividends at contractors the Pentagon judges to be underperforming (Reuters, Feb 6). No opened page says Lockheed was named. In February Taiclet said only that all capital options would be evaluated (Breaking Defense).
  • Rates: the 10-year is near 5% (Fed Desk). No direct read on Lockheed's debt cost found.

People and governance

  • CEO: Jim Taiclet, CEO since June 15, 2020. Not a founder; he came from the board and had run American Tower (Air & Space Forces Magazine).
  • CFO: Evan Scott, since April 17, 2025, a 26-year company finance executive. He replaced Jay Malave, who left for other opportunities (company release).
  • Successor named: Not found.
  • Chair/CEO: Combined. The 2026 proxy carried a shareholder proposal for an independent chair, which the board opposed (Quartr summary). A PRI page lists 35.83% in favour, but the year of that vote is unclear.
  • Control: Dual-class shares or a controlling holder: not found.
  • Recent changes or investigations: Aeronautics president Greg Ulmer retired; OJ Sanchez took over June 1, 2026. On Nov 4, 2026 the Justice Department and EEOC announced a settlement of about $29.5M over alleged race- and sex-based pay and promotion practices. These are allegations only; Lockheed denies them and admits no liability.
  • How much rests on one person: Moderate. Three titles sit with Taiclet and no successor is named, but the business runs on long government contracts.
