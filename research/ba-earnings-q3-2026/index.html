BA earnings — Boeing, Q3 2026

October 27, 202612 min readOpen in the reader

Management sees a record order book and a slow, steady climb in output, and the thing that could spoil it is a factory that still cannot build 737 wings fast enough to turn those orders into cash.

Reported 2026-10-27. Next report: not found. Updated 2026-11-12. Research, not investment advice.

The short version

  • Demand: Stronger. Record backlog of $745.4B and 300 net commercial orders in Q3.
  • Guidance: Cut in effect. No formal guidance, but the cash targets moved out about a year: free cash flow of about $2B in 2026, $3B in 2027, $6B in 2028 and $10B in 2029.
  • Biggest worry: Production stability: 737 wings, the climb to 52 a month, 777X certification by mid-2027.
  • Chart: Leans down. A daily close above $185 ends that.

The numbers

  • Revenue $24,188M, up 4%, against about $23.9B expected: a beat.
  • Core loss per share $0.34 against a $0.25 loss expected: a miss. Core is Boeing's adjusted figure. The GAAP loss was smaller at $0.26, so do not set the GAAP number against the estimate.
  • Free cash flow $464M.

What management said about the road ahead

  • Backlog: Commercial $611.8B (over 6,300 airplanes), defense a record $101B, services $33B.
  • 737: Still stabilizing at 47 a month, expected to take into next year. 52 a month is targeted for the second half of 2027. 2026 deliveries will fall short of the 500 target.
  • 787: Stable at 8 a month; the move to 10 starts this year and is paced by engine deliveries. 2026 deliveries of 90 to 100, nearer the low end.
  • 777X: First delivery still expected in 2027. The financial plan assumes certification by mid-2027.
  • Cash: The CFO called it roughly a one-year shift to the right, with the slope and endpoint unchanged.
  • Airlines: None has replanned orders so far, though growth orders could soften.
  • Tone: "I'm confident we're on the right track." (Kelly Ortberg, President and CEO, earnings call).

What management is worried about

  1. 737 rate stability at 47 a month, with wings the main constraint.
  2. 777X certification timing: mid-2027 is built into the cash plan.
  3. Engine supply for the 787, holding the move from 8 to 10 a month.
  4. Softer airline growth orders if fuel stays high.

The big worry: production stability, both ways

What would ease it

  • Wings catching up. Boeing said in September it would add wing capacity (company feature, Sep 16).
  • The new Everett line. Leeham News reports it adds five a month on top of Renton's 47, with deliveries expected to start early 2027.
  • Certification is clearing: the Max 7 was certified in early August and the Max 10 in October (Leeham).

What would make it worse

  • Deliveries stuck near today's pace. Leeham counts 367 737s delivered in nine months, about 41 a month, well under the 47 being built toward.
  • The regulator. The FAA capped output at 38 a month after January 2024, lifted that in October 2025 and reviewed the line before approving 47 (Seattle Times via Aviation Pros). Higher rates need the Everett line's production certification.
  • A 777X slip past mid-2027. Boeing took another charge on the program in Q3.
  • Airlines trimming growth. Delta is holding Q4 seat growth under 2% and paying about $4.25 a gallon; United pays about $4.80.

How this company is placed against it

  • The order book is not the problem: over 6,300 airplanes.
  • The balance sheet is the weak spot: $45.9B of debt against $20.7B of cash and securities, with the 10-year near 5%.
  • Against peers, Lockheed and RTX raised free cash flow this quarter. Boeing pushed its $10B out to 2029.

Money plans

  • 2027 capital spending about $5B, about $1B higher, for Charleston, St. Louis and the Navy F/A-XX fighter; about $4B a year after.
  • Debt flat at $45.9B; cash and securities $20.7B.
  • Dividends and buybacks: not discussed.

People and governance

  • CEO: Kelly Ortberg, President and CEO since 2024 (2026 proxy). Not a founder.
  • CFO: Jay Malave, since Aug 15, 2025, formerly Lockheed Martin's finance chief. He replaced Brian West (CFO Dive).
  • Chair: Steven Mollenkopf, independent chair since 2024. Boeing's by-laws require an independent chair, so the roles are split.
  • Successor named: No. The proxy says a board committee oversees senior management succession.
  • Control: Dual-class shares or a controlling holder: not found on the pages opened.
  • Recent changes: Brad Tilden joined the board in December 2025 (proxy). No executive departure in the last 12 months was found.
  • Open oversight: A May 2025 non-prosecution agreement with the Department of Justice, which the board's audit committee reviews. NPR reported in November 2025 that a judge dismissed the criminal charge over the 737 Max crashes; Boeing agreed to pay or invest a further $1.1B and may pick its own compliance consultant, and some families planned to appeal. FAA review of each 737 rate increase continues.
  • How much rests on one person: Less on a person than on the factory. The CEO and CFO are both recent hires and the chair is independent.
