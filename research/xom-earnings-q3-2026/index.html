XOM earnings — Exxon Mobil, Q3 2026

October 30, 202614 min readOpen in the reader

Management sees tight fuel markets and rich refining margins lasting well into next year, and the thing that could spoil it is the same Middle East shutdown that is already cutting Exxon's own barrels.

Reported 2026-10-30. Next report: not found (management pointed to a call in late January). Updated 2026-11-12. Research, not investment advice.

The short version

  • Demand: Stronger. Refining margins are above the 10-year range and international gas prices are above it too. Chemicals are the weak spot, below the bottom of the range.
  • Guidance: Held, and extended a year. $20B of buybacks now planned for 2027 as well as 2026. 2026 cash capex stays at $27–29B.
  • Biggest worry: The Middle East shutdown. Lost volumes cost Upstream $860M in Q3 and $2,140M so far this year.
  • Chart: No lean yet. Price sits just under your 2.0 ext at $166.89. A daily close above it leans up; a daily close under your 1.618 at $159.09 leans down.

The numbers

  • GAAP EPS $3.72. EPS excluding identified items and timing effects $4.23.
  • Consensus was $4.00 (Webull) or $3.90 (Earnings Whispers). So it is a miss on the GAAP figure and a beat on the adjusted one. Webull shows a miss only because it sets GAAP against an adjusted estimate.
  • Revenue $113.2B against a $102.9B estimate (Webull). Production 4,496 thousand barrels of oil equivalent a day, slightly below Q2.

What management said about the road ahead

  • Product markets: CEO Darren Woods said about a third of the product shortage is clean fuel that cannot leave the Middle East, with roughly half of Russia's refining capacity offline. He sees normalisation running well through next year.
  • The Strait: He expects the Strait of Hormuz to reopen and gave no date.
  • Margins: Refining above the 10-year range. Chemicals below the bottom of it. International gas above the range on limited Middle East supply.
  • Growth projects: Guyana's fifth project (Uaru) is expected to start in Q4. Golden Pass LNG Train 2 is due to be substantially complete by December 2026, Train 3 mechanically complete in mid-2027.
  • Costs: $16.9B of structural savings since 2019, against a $20B target by 2030.
  • 2027 plan: No December plan event this year. The 2027 figures are to be reaffirmed by press release this quarter.
  • Dividend: "we're not going to ever cut our dividend." (Darren Woods, Chairman and CEO)

What management is worried about

  1. Middle East volume losses. $860M off Upstream earnings in Q3, $2,140M year to date. The 10-Q cites restrictions on supply through the Strait of Hormuz and damage at Qatar facilities.
  2. Chemical margins. Below the bottom of the 10-year range.
  3. Price swings. No planning price was given; the filing only flags volatility.
  4. EU methane rules. Exxon said it would stop importing into Europe without a workable way to comply.
  5. Golden Pass. A contractor bankruptcy affects the project; Train 2 is still guided to December 2026.
  6. Venezuela terms. Named as a risk; no figure given.

The 10-Q's risk section points back to the 2025 10-K. Whether anything was added or reworded: not found.

The big worry: the Middle East shutdown, both ways

What would ease it

  • The Strait reopens. QatarEnergy's CEO said in September that Qatar could be back to normal operations within a few weeks of a reopening.
  • Workarounds keep growing. The EIA's October outlook has shut-in Middle East oil falling from 10.9 million barrels a day at the May peak to 4.5 million in Q4, with most of the region back to pre-conflict output by the end of Q2 2027.
  • Escorts keep working. US Central Command said last month it had helped Gulf countries export 1.5 billion barrels through Hormuz since the summer.
  • While it lasts, Exxon's refineries and its gas outside the Gulf earn more, which offsets part of the lost volume.

What would make it worse

  • More attacks on shipping. On Nov 10–11, Iran's forces reported several tankers on fire in the Strait, one after hitting a mine (Euronews).
  • Slow repairs in Qatar. Two damaged LNG trains at Ras Laffan will take three years to repair, per QatarEnergy's CEO. Whether those are trains Exxon has a share in: not found.
  • A longer shutdown feeds inflation. The Fed says energy is now leaking into core prices, and futures put about 90% odds on a December rate rise. Higher rates lean on fuel demand.
  • The catch for you to hold in mind: a reopening also ends the windfall. The EIA sees Brent at $105 in Q4 2026, $87 by Q2 2027 and $74 by Q4 2027. Chevron warned of petrochemical oversupply once flows normalise. So the relief on volumes arrives with lower prices and thinner refining margins.

How this company is placed against it

  • It is spread out. Guyana, the Permian and Golden Pass on the US Gulf Coast do not pass through Hormuz.
  • The balance sheet is light: debt $34.3B, cash $12.2B, net debt $22.1B, against $9.4B of payouts in the quarter.
  • Versus peers: Exxon is the only one of the three producers in your docs that put a dollar figure on its Middle East losses. Chevron says its physical operations are not materially affected so far. Occidental ties lower international sales to the conflict but has hedges and a $50 planning price.

Money plans

  • Capital spending: Q3 capex $7.0B; $20.0B in nine months of a $27–29B year.
  • Buybacks: $5.1B in Q3; $20B planned for 2026 and again for 2027, assuming reasonable market conditions.
  • Dividend: $4.3B paid in Q3. Q4 dividend raised 4% to $1.07 a share.
  • Debt: $34.3B, with $12.2B of cash.

People and governance

  • CEO: Darren Woods, age 60, CEO since the start of 2017 (MarketScreener) and also chairman. Not a founder; a career Exxon executive.
  • CFO: Neil Hansen, since Feb 1, 2026. He replaced Kathryn Mikells, who retired for health reasons (CFO Dive, Dec 2025). So the finance chief is in his first year.
  • Successor named: Not found. No CEO succession plan was named in the pages read.
  • Control: No controlling holder or dual-class shares found. One vote per share. The chair and CEO roles are combined; a shareholder proposal to split them got 15.2% at the 2026 meeting. Lead independent director: not found.
  • Recent changes: Shareholders approved moving the company's legal home from New Jersey to Texas with 71.2% in favour. Exxon also runs a retail voting programme that casts enrolled holders' votes with the board unless they override; a proposal to modify it got 23.5%. Manifest (a proxy adviser, April 2026) reported that the City of Hollywood Police Officers' Retirement System sued Exxon and its directors over the programme, alleging breach of fiduciary duty. Outcome: not found.
  • Investigations: None found in the last 12 months.
  • How much rests on one person: Moderate. Woods holds both top jobs and nearly 85% of votes backed that, but this is a deep-bench company with a 13-person board. The thing to watch is that the board's hand is getting stronger relative to holders (Texas move, retail voting), not weaker.
