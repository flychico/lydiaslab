Desk rules: technical analysis, scoring, edge and risk

20 min readOpen in the reader

Adopted Nov 19, 2026 and upgraded the same day. Every report, intraday read, scorecard update and Friday review follows this document together with voice.md, play-scorecard.md, technical-read.md and chart-read-now.md. Where this document and older scorecard rules disagree, this document wins. Change only after a scored Friday or monthly review. Log every change at the bottom with a new version number.

1. Technical analysis hierarchy (mandatory for every play and every important chart discussion)

Every setup and every technical paragraph is built in this order and explained in plain language:

  1. Market structure, trend and direction: higher highs and higher lows (uptrend) or lower highs and lower lows (downtrend) on the relevant timeframe (his daily, 2-day, 3-day and 4-day charts; technical-read.md); the trend from the 50-day and 200-day; the direction from the 14-day and 30-day (section 13). A play whose direction the 14/30 does not confirm stops here.
  2. Key levels: his Fibs (from fib-style.md, never changed), clear horizontal demand and supply, moving-average clusters, prior day and week highs and lows. Levels near current price first (chart-read-now.md).
  3. Confirmation: at least TWO of:
    • Volume above the 20-day average on the break or rejection
    • Multi-timeframe agreement (the higher-timeframe bias plus the daily trigger)
    • Relative strength vs the S&P 500 or the sector ETF
    • A clear rejection or failed auction at the level
    • A Fib + moving-average cluster at the entry (section 13, rule 5)
  4. Only then the trigger, the stop, the targets and the R:R. Triggers and stops are intraday prices the desk can act on during market hours, confirmed by an intraday candle (usually 15-minute); never "on the close" (the desk, Nov 19, 2026). This replaces the close-based triggers and stops in play-scorecard.md for new plays.

A pure Fib trigger with only one weak confirmation is not allowed. Every play shows a one-line confluence note (e.g. "direction ▼ (14 under 30) · trend up (50 over 200) · broke your 1.618 $213.52 · volume").

2. Before any play is listed (WATCHING or OPEN)

Show in one compact block:

  • Edge on file: the setup type's line from the Edge Registry, labelled "early read" until 20 closed plays
  • Direction and trend: direction from the 14/30 (with the date of the cross), trend from the 50/200, and whether the S&P 500's own 14/30 allows it (section 13)
  • R:R from the actual proposed entry to the target used, and the option's own R:R (section 12, rule 5)
  • Liquidity: spread ≤ 10% of mid, open interest ≥ 500, about 50+ traded last session
  • IV vs realized: CHEAP / FAIR / RICH / EXPENSIVE with the ratio (section 12)
  • Earnings: "earnings inside" or "none inside"
  • Thesis broken if: one plain condition, written at birth, that ends the idea whatever the price stop says
  • Event risk: any big catalyst before the next close the play is tied to (a peer's earnings, CPI, the Fed, the weekly EIA report for oil names). Example: "PLTR put has high NVDA beta into tonight's print."
  • Confluence: structure + level + the confirmations from section 1

3. Hard limits on the book (paper)

  • No new setup types until the existing ones each have ≥ 20 closed plays and positive expectancy. Existing types: breakout, reclaim of old floor, uptrend + RSI<40, Fib flip pullback, trend continuation (calls and puts), level failure put, breakdown, failed rally put, breakout over old high, and Fib break + 14/30 cross (calls and puts; the desk’s setup, added Nov 20, 2026, section 13).
  • Maximum 8 OPEN + 4 active WATCHING. Everything else goes to a "Parking lot" list on the scorecard, one line each; an idea moves up only when a slot opens.
  • Open heat cap: $2,500 (paper dollars lost if every OPEN play hits its stop, from the latest marks). If a new play would push the heat over the cap, the scorecard picks the stronger idea, swaps out the weakest open play, or skips. (the desk to confirm or change the number.) Heat is scorecard bookkeeping only; it never appears in a report (voice.md rule 11).
  • Correlation gate: if 3 or more OPEN plays share the same primary driver (e.g. AI/chip shorts, oil, travel), new ideas in that group are blocked unless they replace an existing one.
  • Every scorecard update and every report's plays section carries a one-line mix note (e.g. "7 of 9 ideas are puts; 4 are AI-linked").

3b. Removed names

  • Removed names leave every report at once (the desk, Nov 19, 2026, after the Nov 19 Debrief still charted V and set a V alert: V and others "we talked about removing from the watch list since the option chain did not align with our investment style"). A ticker that is off claude/watchlist.md never appears in a report again: no chart, card, alert, scan row, play or idea. Any paper play still open on a removed name closes at the next intraday read or nightly mark, at that run's mid, noted "closed: name removed from the watchlist" (due Nov 20: V Dec 18 $405 P and HLT Dec 18 $380 C). This replaces rule 6 in claude/watchlist.md. Before writing any report, check every ticker against the watchlist's tickers: line.

4. Scorecard header (first lines of every update, in this order)

  1. Open heat at stops (paper $) and the cap
  2. Closed sample size, win rate, average win, average loss, expectancy per play
  3. Setup-type leaderboard by expectancy (from the Edge Registry)
  4. Cancellation rate: NO TRADE ideas ÷ all ideas listed (target under 30%)
  5. Average R:R of the plays that triggered (from the trigger price)
  6. Correlation / lean note
  7. Gate blocks this week (v1.4): ideas blocked by the IV rules (section 12) and by section 13, so the Friday review can see what the rules kept out and how those names traded. Fib break + 14/30 cross signals are counted on their own line, taken or not.

5. Reports (pre-market and nightly), still readable

  • Top 3 actionable sit right under the "What mattered today / What matters this morning" list: the idea, why now in one clause, R:R, liquidity OK, and which of the day's forces it lines up with. The rest of the order in voice.md does not change.
  • Plays-check table, always these columns (layout locked in claude/premarket_build.py): idea · current mid · distance to stop (%) · distance to target (%) · R:R from here (stock, and option in brackets) · IV/RV label and ratio · status (IN THE ENTRY ZONE / WAITING / TOO LATE / INVALIDATED, voice.md rule 11). An idea held back by section 12 or 13 shows WAITING with the reason in a few words ("WAITING: EXPENSIVE 1.31", "WAITING: 14-day still over the 30-day", "WAITING: S&P direction is down").
  • Opening rule of the day (pre-market): one concrete behavior, one sentence.
  • Technical discussion follows the hierarchy in section 1 but is written in the normal desk-partner voice.
  • Fed, Beige Book and bank-target notes end with 1–2 lines on what they mean for the current ideas by name.

6. Personal skill track (how the desk improves)

Every Weekly Coaching (and most Daily Debriefs) contains:

  • One clear technical teaching moment, in the normal voice.
  • One short deliberate-practice note for the desk, done on his own charts and timeframes (example: "Before tomorrow's open, mark every failed auction on the daily and 2-day charts of the names we are watching. We will compare.").
  • On Friday, a brief honest note on the most common chart-reading gap that showed up that week (his or ours) and how we close it.

The goal is that the daily emails themselves train his eye and his process.

7. Scoring every closed play + Edge Registry

Each closed play gets a short post-mortem paragraph (the thesis, what happened, whether the level and confirmations held up, one rule change or "none") and a score:

  • Thesis accuracy 0–2 · Risk framing 0–2 · Process followed 0–2 · Clarity/actionability 0–2 · Expectancy contribution (+ / − / 0)

The Edge Registry (section 10) is updated after every closed play and fully reviewed every 10 closed plays of a type. Any setup that reaches 20 closed plays with a win rate under 55% or negative expectancy is downgraded (half the slots, an extra confirmation) or retired. The change is logged.

8. Masters checklist (every Friday)

  1. Druckenmiller, liquidity: what did the Fed, the 2-year and the dollar do this week, and does the book lean with or against it?
  2. Griffin, sizing: is any single play more than 25% of the open heat? Is the heat under the cap?
  3. Tudor Jones, defense first: did every play have its stop and thesis-broken line from day one, and were they honored?
  4. Marks, the cycle: are we buying what everyone is buying? One line on sentiment (AAII, put/call) against our lean.
  5. Simons, the data: which setup types have the sample to trust, and which are still early reads?
  6. Price structure: what did price structure teach us this week?
  7. Price of the option (v1.4): what did the ideas pay for volatility this week (average IV/RV of everything listed), and how did the ones blocked as RICH or EXPENSIVE trade?

9. Changing the rules

  • Only after a scored Friday or monthly review (first Sunday), never mid-week on one result.
  • The monthly review aggregates scores by setup type, names the 2 rules that helped most and the 2 that hurt most, and proposes specific edits.
  • Proposed changes are tested on the existing closed-play history when the sample allows (10+ closed of that type), showing before and after.
  • Every change gets a new version number and a dated log entry.

12. When options are expensive (v1.4, the desk, Nov 20, 2026: "harder rules when options are expensive")

We only buy options, so the price of volatility is part of every trade. These rules are hard gates, not notes.

  1. The label. IV of the chosen contract ÷ the stock's 20-day realized volatility (annualized close-to-close):
    • CHEAP under 0.80 · FAIR 0.80–1.10 · RICH 1.10–1.25 · EXPENSIVE over 1.25.
    • Always shown with the ratio ("RICH 1.19"). RICH and EXPENSIVE are written in red.
  2. EXPENSIVE = no entry. An EXPENSIVE idea can never be IN THE ENTRY ZONE, whatever the setup. It stays WAITING ("WAITING: EXPENSIVE 1.31") until an intraday read shows the ratio at 1.25 or lower. No exceptions for great-looking charts.
  3. RICH needs more. A RICH idea needs R:R of at least 2.5 to its target (instead of 1.5) and a Fib + moving-average cluster at the entry (section 13, rule 5).
  4. High-volatility market. When the VIX closed the prior day at 25 or higher, every new idea needs R:R of at least 2.0 (2.5 if RICH). At a VIX of 30 or higher, at most 2 ideas can be IN THE ENTRY ZONE at once; the rest wait in order of R:R.
  5. The option's own R:R. A good stock R:R can still be a bad option trade once time decay and IV are paid. Estimate the contract's value:
    • at the target: mid + delta × move + ½ × gamma × move² − theta × days to target (days to target = distance ÷ ATR, rounded up);
    • at the stop: the same formula with the stop's move and one day of theta. The option's R:R = gain at the target ÷ loss at the stop. It must be at least 1.5 (2.0 when the VIX is 25 or higher). If the stock R:R passes and the option's fails, the idea is WAITING. Reports show both: "R:R 2.1 (option 1.7)".
  6. Earnings inside the expiry count as EXPENSIVE whatever the ratio (the crush after the report takes the premium). Such an idea is skipped unless the desk asks for an earnings play by name.
  7. Too far out of the money. A contract with delta under 0.30 is allowed only if its option R:R passes rule 5 with a target move no bigger than 1.5 ATR. Cheap-looking far strikes are where expensive volatility hides.
  8. Liquidity still first: spread over 10% of mid or open interest under 500 is no idea, at any price (section 2).

13. Direction, trend and the Fib levels (v1.5, the desk, Nov 20, 2026)

the desk: "i prefer the idea more of the 14 day and the 30 day moving averages is what determines direction. you can have the 50 day and 200 day determine trend." His NVDA example: "when price was hovering over the 213 level and then finally was under both the 14 day and 30 day moving avg ... our entry should had been there once price broke below the 213 level (fib level) followed by price being below the 14 day as well as the 14 day sma crossing below the 30 day. targets being fib levels and 50 day sma and 200 day sma."

  1. Direction comes from the 14-day and 30-day.
    • Direction down: price under both the 14-day and the 30-day, and the 14-day under the 30-day.
    • Direction up: price over both, and the 14-day over the 30-day.
    • Anything else: no direction; no new idea.
    • The day the 14-day crosses the 30-day is the direction change.
  2. Trend comes from the 50-day and 200-day. Trend up: the 50-day over the 200-day; trend down: the 50-day under it. The trend does not block a trade. It tells us whether the trade runs with the bigger trend or against it, and it puts the 50-day and 200-day on the map as targets. Against-trend ideas say so in their line ("against the uptrend: the 200-day $203 is the first target").
  3. The market check: no new calls while the S&P 500's own direction is down (SPX under its 14-day and 30-day, 14 under 30); no new puts while its direction is up, unless the name's own direction has already turned and it has broken one of his levels. (This replaces the v1.4 200-day market gate; it blocks the Nov 17 XOM call and the Nov 18 AAPL call too.)
  4. The setup: Fib break + 14/30 cross (puts; calls are the mirror):
    • The level breaks: price trades through one of his Fib levels (the desk's on a name without his) and a 15-minute candle closes on the far side.
    • Direction confirms: price is under the 14-day and the 30-day, and the 14-day is under the 30-day (it has crossed, or crosses within the next few sessions). The order can be either way: if the cross came first, the level break is the trigger.
    • Entry: the first 15-minute candle with all of it true. If price has already run to the next target by then, the idea is TOO LATE.
    • Stop: a 15-minute candle closing back over the 30-day (puts) or under it (calls), or the 14-day crossing back over the 30-day, whichever comes first. A bounce back over the 14-day alone is a warning, not the exit: on NVDA's Oct 28 bounce the first 15-minute candle closed $218.99, 8 cents over that day's 14-day ($218.91), and a 14-day stop would have taken the trade out at the top of the bounce; it never got near the 30-day ($224.44). In the backtest the 30-day stop and the 14-day stop paid almost the same (+0.30R vs +0.33R per trade).
    • Targets, in order of distance: his next Fib levels plus the 50-day and 200-day when they sit in the path. Use the nearest one that pays at least 2 to 1 against the stop (2.5 if RICH, section 12); the nearer ones are the "watch for a stall" levels. If none pays 2 to 1, there is no idea.
  5. Fib + average cluster: a Fib level with one of the 14/30/50/200-day averages within 0.5 ATR of it is a cluster. A cluster counts as one of the two confirmations in section 1, and a cluster in the path is the most likely place for a stall.
  6. In the reports: each idea's line shows direction (with the cross date), trend and the level, e.g. "direction ▼ since Oct 23 (14 under 30) · against the uptrend (50 over 200) · broke your 1.618 $213.52 · stop: back over the 30-day ($224) · stall zone: your 1.382 $201.24 / 200-day $203.36 cluster · target: your 0.786 $170.22". The "Every name, scanned" table's Tr column shows the direction arrow (▲ / ▼ / ◆ from the 14/30) and the trend in words.
  7. NVDA, Oct–Nov 2026, worked through his way: price sat on his 1.618 ($213.52) for a week. Oct 22 it closed $212.21, under the level and under the 14-day ($225.04) and 30-day ($224.27). Oct 23 the 14-day ($223.05) crossed under the 30-day ($224.03) with price $210.96: the entry. The 50-day ($222.39) was over the 200-day ($203.36), so this was a put against the uptrend. The bounce that followed topped at $219.99 on Oct 28, poking over the 14-day that morning but never near the 30-day ($224.44), and the 14-day never crossed back. Oct 30 it gapped down. The 200-day (about $204) and his 1.382 ($201.24) were hit Oct 30 to Nov 4, his 0.786 ($170.22) on Nov 10 and his 0.5 ($155.33) on Nov 16. From $210.96 with the 30-day stop (about $224, $13 of risk), the 200-day / 1.382 cluster paid only 0.6–0.8 to 1, so it is the stall zone; his 0.786 paid about 3.1 to 1, which is the target rule 4 picks.
  8. Backtest (daily bars, about 6 months to Nov 20, 2026; his ladders on NVDA, PLTR, AMZN, XOM, BE, TSLA, DAL, OXY, AMD, BA, CRDO): 50 signals (a close through one of his levels within 7 sessions of a 14/30 cross the same way, price beyond both averages).
    • Direction: the first target came before the swing-high stop 30 times out of 45 resolved (67%). The signal finds direction.
    • Payoff: the cross comes late, so the nearest target was usually close; the median R:R to it was about 0.5 to 0.6, and taking it every time made about +0.1R per trade, a wash once option decay is paid.
    • Stop at the 30-day and the second target: 20 wins in 44 resolved, median R:R about 2.0, about +0.30R per trade with wins capped at 5R (the 14-day stop: +0.33R). That is why rule 4 skips targets that don't pay 2 to 1.
    • Waiting for a retest of the broken level made it worse: 5 wins in 19 (about −0.5R per trade). When price came back to his level, it usually took it back. No retest entries.
    • Limits: his ladders were drawn in November and fitted to past turning points, so levels that line up with history flatter the test; daily closes stand in for 15-minute candles; no option prices. The live record in the Edge Registry is what counts.
