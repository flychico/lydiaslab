FOMC Minutes — May 6–7, 2025

May 7, 20253 min readOpen in the reader

Official minutes: HTML · PDF Local PDF: 2025-05-07 FOMC Minutes.pdf

Decision

The Committee held the federal funds target range at 4-1/4 to 4-1/2 percent, with interest on reserve balances at 4.4 percent and the primary credit rate at 4.5 percent. The vote was unanimous, 12-0: Powell, Williams, Barr, Bowman, Collins, Cook, Goolsbee, Jefferson, Kashkari (voting as an alternate), Kugler, Musalem and Waller. Runoff continued at the slower pace set in March: a $5 billion monthly cap on Treasuries and $35 billion on agency debt and MBS.

Economic outlook

The first-quarter GDP advance estimate showed a slight decline, which staff said was likely distorted by a surge of imports brought forward ahead of tariffs. Real private domestic final purchases still rose solidly. Staff's GDP projection for 2025–2026 was weaker than in March. Participants said activity "continued to expand at a solid pace" and that consumer spending and business investment were solid, but business and consumer sentiment had deteriorated sharply.

Inflation

Total PCE inflation was 2.3 percent in March and core PCE 2.6 percent, both lower than a year earlier. Short-term inflation expectations rose, while longer-term expectations were described as well anchored, though some participants saw a risk of upward drift. Staff expected tariffs to boost inflation markedly in 2025 and less in 2026, before inflation returned to 2 percent by 2027.

Labor market

Unemployment was 4.2 percent in March and April. Payroll gains over those two months were solid and roughly in line with the 2024 pace. The vacancies-to-unemployed ratio was 1.0 in April. Wage growth slowed: the employment cost index rose 3.4 percent over the year to March and average hourly earnings 3.8 percent over the year to April. Staff expected unemployment to move above its natural rate by year-end and stay there through 2027.

Financial markets & balance sheet

Longer-maturity Treasury yields rose and the broad dollar fell more than 2 percent. Credit spreads widened. Equities ended roughly unchanged over the period but below their mid-February peak. Markets priced roughly one to three rate cuts by year-end, depending on the measure. Money markets were orderly and reserves, above $3.2 trillion, were described as abundant. Survey respondents expected runoff to end around January 2026. Early settlement was to become a regular feature of the standing repo facility from late June.

Risks participants flagged

  • Tariffs were the dominant concern; increases were significantly larger and broader than expected.
  • Uncertainty was described as unusually elevated.
  • Risks of both higher unemployment and higher inflation were judged to have risen.
  • Almost all participants worried that inflation could prove more persistent.
  • Financial stability: hedge fund leverage, private credit and Treasury market resilience.

What it signaled about next moves

The Committee said it was "well positioned to wait for more clarity" on the outlook for inflation and economic activity. It would carefully assess incoming data and the balance of risks, and was ready to adjust policy if risks emerged that could impede its goals. With risks to both sides of its mandate rising, the message was that no move was imminent.

Read-through for SPY

  • With the Fed pledging to wait, investors could not lean on near-term rate cuts after the spring tariff shock.
  • Staff flagging a rise in unemployment and a tariff-driven inflation bump pointed to a stagflation-lite backdrop that weighs on equity multiples.
  • Equities had recovered to roughly flat over the period, showing markets were tolerating policy uncertainty as long as growth held up.
