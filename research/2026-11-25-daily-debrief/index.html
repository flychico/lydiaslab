Lesson of the day: A target that trades is a target

GOOGL hit the put idea's first target, $265, in the first two hours. It did it cleanly: four 15-minute closes under the line and a low of $262.73. The put traded as high as $15.57. By the close it was $9.70.

Two things took the money back. The obvious one is price: GOOGL rallied 3.9% off its low. The quieter one is volatility: the VIX traded far above its 28.29 close in the morning, and when fear drains out of the market, puts lose value even if the stock stands still.

This is the second time in four sessions. On Nov 20 the PLTR put idea closed 0.3 ATR from its target and gave back $795 the next day. The pattern is the same: we planned the entry to the cent and left the exit to a schedule.

The fix is simple and it is already on the list for Friday's rules review: when target 1 trades on a 15-minute close, the idea takes the profit at that moment. Holding past the target is a new trade, and it must pass the same R:R test from that price. From $265 with a stop at $285.20, GOOGL paid 0.7 to 1. Nobody would open that trade, so nobody should keep it.

Practice: for each idea in the plays section, set a price alert at target 1 tonight, not just at the trigger. When it rings, the decision is already made.
