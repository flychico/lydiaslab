KO earnings — Coca-Cola, Q3 2026

October 27, 202613 min readOpen in the reader

Management sees steady growth carried by overseas volume and a raised outlook, and the thing that could spoil it is the shopper at home, who is hunting for value while North America volume sits flat.

Reported Oct 27, 2026. Next report: not found (one calendar site estimates Feb 8, 2027, unconfirmed). Updated Nov 12, 2026. Research, not investment advice.

The short version

  • Demand: steady. Volume grew 5% worldwide (India, China, Mexico, Brazil) and was flat in North America.
  • Guidance: raised. Organic revenue growth at least 5% (was about 5%); comparable EPS growth at least 11% (was 9–10%); free cash flow at least $13.0B (was about $12.4B).
  • Biggest worry: the value-hunting shopper in North America, with restaurants weak and mix eating most of the price increases.
  • Chart: leans up. Price is at a 52-week high; the level that decides it is $91.48 (the old August ceiling area). These are my lines, not yours.

The numbers

  • Revenue $13.35B against $12.93B expected. A beat.
  • GAAP EPS $0.93 against $0.88 (Webull). Comparable EPS $0.91 against $0.87 (Zacks). A beat on both bases; compare like with like, since Webull's "actual" is GAAP and the Zacks pair is adjusted.
  • Price/mix was only +1%: about 4 points of pricing, less about 3 points of weaker mix.

What management said about the road ahead

  • Rest of 2026: outlook raised on every line except capital spending, which stays at about $2.2B.
  • 2027: a slight currency headwind and an 8% hit to reported revenue from selling bottlers. Full guidance comes with Q4 results.
  • Next quarter: Q4 has six fewer days than a year ago, and logistics costs are flagged.
  • Pricing: value matters in every market and income group, and management expects that to last into 2027. "value continues to be something that cuts across every market and every consumer." Henrique Braun, CEO.
  • Channels: restaurants are weak; at-home is stronger.
  • Costs: lower input costs helped Europe; higher input costs hurt North America.
  • North America: flat volume against +3% in Q2. Management pointed to July 4 timing falling into Q2 and to supply limits on Topo Chico. Trademark Coca-Cola grew; water, sports drinks, coffee and tea fell.

What management is worried about

  1. Affordability and mix. About 3 points of mix drag against 4 points of pricing; North America volume flat.
  2. The IRS tax dispute. About $14B of potential further tax and interest for 2010–2025, plus about $1.4B for the first nine months of 2026 (10-Q). Not in guidance. The appeal was argued June 25, 2026; no ruling found.
  3. Restaurants and other away-from-home sales. Called weak; no figure given.
  4. Costs. Input costs up in North America; logistics costs flagged for Q4.
  5. Currency and bottler sales. An 8% revenue hit in 2027 from refranchising, plus a slight currency headwind.

The big worry: the value-hunting shopper at home, both ways

What would ease it

  • Cheaper fuel. AAA's national average was $3.94 on Nov 11, down from $4.36 a month earlier. October averaged $4.31, the highest October on record (EIA figures, via a Nov 9 article).
  • A better mood. University of Michigan sentiment rose to 51.0 in early November from 48.0, and it rose for lower- and middle-wealth households.
  • Restaurants stabilising. McDonald's US sales fell 0.2% in Q3 but turned positive in September and October.
  • New business. Coca-Cola is to supply KFC's US restaurants from early 2027, replacing PepsiCo (Atlanta Journal-Constitution).
  • Supply fixes and a new North America president (Rob Gehring, from Dec 1).

What would make it worse

  • More rate hikes. The Fed desk doc shows December priced for a hike, and the Beige Book says customers are resisting price increases and margins are squeezed.
  • Restaurant traffic keeps falling. US fast-food traffic was down 2.6% in Q3 (Revenue Management Solutions, via Nation's Restaurant News).
  • Food-benefit limits on soda. 23 states have approval to restrict soda purchases with SNAP benefits; Circana found benefit-funded shopping trips that included soft drinks fell 8 points on the year.
  • A rival taking the growth. Keurig Dr Pepper's US soft-drink volume/mix rose 7.2% in Q3 while PepsiCo's North America beverage volume fell 2%. The category is not shrinking evenly.
  • Oil turning back up. The energy pages in this project describe Hormuz restrictions that are not yet resolved.

How this company is placed against it

  • It still has pricing: about 4 points taken this quarter, and North America operating income rose 5% on flat volume.
  • The growth is overseas, so one weak region does not sink the year. That is why guidance went up anyway.
  • Balance sheet is light for a staples company: net debt about 1.3x EBITDA. The IRS case is the one item that could change that picture.

Money plans

  • Capital spending about $2.2B, unchanged.
  • Dividend $0.53 a quarter. Buybacks only to offset dilution.
  • Selling part of its Africa bottling stake by year-end.
  • $6.0B was already paid to the IRS in September 2024 for 2007–2009; the reserve for the rest is $538M.

People and governance

  • CEO: Henrique Braun, since March 31, 2026. Joined the company in 1996; not a founder.
  • Previous CEO: James Quincey moved to Executive Chairman (2026 proxy). Whether he still holds the role this month: not found on a November page.
  • CFO: John Murphy, CFO since 2019 and President since 2022. He has also been interim head of North America since Aug 1.
  • Successor named: not found. A board committee owns CEO succession planning.
  • Control: one class of stock, no controlling holder found. Largest holder is Berkshire Hathaway at 9.29%.
  • Chair: split from the CEO, but the chair is an executive, not independent. Lead independent director: David Weinberg.
  • Recent changes: Jennifer Mann stepped down as North America president on Aug 1 with no reason given; Rob Gehring (most recently at Monster Energy) takes over Dec 1. Investigations: not found.
  • How much rests on one person: little. But note the timing: a new CEO, a vacancy in the weakest region, and the CFO covering three jobs until December.
