Lesson of the day: A peer's earnings can move your stock before its own report does

DELL lost 11.8% today because of HPE, not because of Dell. HPE reported Monday night and warned that memory and components stay scarce into late 2028 and that AI server margins shrink next year. Dell sells the same kind of server to the same customers, so investors priced HPE's warning into Dell a day early: $39.63 a share, before Dell said a word.

Then Dell's own numbers moved it the other way, about +$12.4 after hours. Two reports, two moves, in opposite directions inside 24 hours.

Why this matters for an options buyer: the weekly DELL straddle at $295 cost about $39.50 this afternoon, a bet on a 13.4% move. After hours the stock moved about 4%. Implied volatility near 190% will collapse tomorrow, and a call buyer who was right on direction still loses most of the premium. That is the earnings crush, and the read-through day (HPE's report) is when the crush got priced in.

The rule we already use: every idea carries an "event risk" line, and a peer's report counts. Before Dell's report, the event line for any AI-server idea should have named HPE on Monday night. Tonight the same logic says: any NVDA idea names the 10-Q (Dec 4); any IWM or bank idea names PCE tomorrow at 8:30.

Practice: for each idea in the plays section, write down the one report, peer or data release that could move it before our first target. If you can't name one, look harder.
