AMZN earnings — Amazon, Q3 2026

October 29, 202613 min readOpen in the reader

Management sees AWS growing above 40% for two more years with capacity used the moment it lands, and the thing that could spoil it is that the build is being paid for with debt while the AWS margin is not promised to hold.

Reported 2026-10-29. Next report: not found (Webull lists no date yet). Updated 2026-11-12. Research, not investment advice.

The short version

  • Demand: stronger in AWS (+39.3%); steady in retail (online stores +2%, ads +11%).
  • Guidance: held. Q4 sales $240–247B (+12–16%), operating income $28.5–33.5B. 2026 cash capital spending about $220B, held; 2027 to "meaningfully increase", no figure.
  • Biggest worry: a debt-funded build. Trailing free cash flow is negative $42.4B and long-term debt went from $65.6B to $173.1B this year.
  • Chart: leans up. Your 2.382 extension at $264.43 is the level that decides it; your 2.618 at $275.22 is the lid.

The numbers

  • Net sales $201.94B (+12%) against a $202.28B estimate; EPS $1.89 against $1.96. Both slight misses.
  • EPS fell from $1.95 mainly because last year's quarter held $9.5B of gains on its Anthropic stake. That is an investment gain, not the business.
  • Operating income $28.1B, a 13.9% margin and a company record, above the top of July's $22.5–26.5B guide.

What management said about the road ahead

  • Next quarter: sales $240–247B; operating income $28.5–33.5B against $25.0B a year ago.
  • AWS growth: Andy Jassy projects growth above 40% in both 2027 and 2028.
  • Backlog: about $578B, up from $496B in July, with a 6.1-year average life. Two customers stand out in the filing: an OpenAI expansion of $100B over 8 years and an Anthropic expansion of more than $100B over 10 years.
  • Supply: capacity is used as soon as it lands. Jassy said an AI accelerator is being installed roughly every 14 seconds, and power and data center space are booked years ahead.
  • Margins: AWS operating margin about 38.7%. The CFO would not commit to it lasting, citing thinner AI margins and memory costs.
  • Own chips: commitments for Graviton and Trainium are each up more than 20-fold; Trainium goes on sale outside AWS from late 2027.
  • Retail: slower. Prime Day moved mostly into Q2.

What management is worried about

  1. Getting the money back. The filing says returns on capital spending may not be recouped; $175.7B was spent in nine months.
  2. Borrowing costs and dilution. Interest expense $1.7B against $538M a year ago; more financing expected.
  3. AWS margin. About 38.7% today, with no promise it holds.
  4. Memory supply and cost. July's raise in the 2026 spending plan (from about $200B to about $220B) was put down to memory-chip costs.
  5. Few chip suppliers and energy shortages. Both named in the filing.
  6. Hard-to-value private stakes. $50.0B put into OpenAI preferred stock, on top of the Anthropic holding.

The big worry: a debt-funded build, both ways

What would ease it

  • Operating income landing in the $28.5–33.5B Q4 range or above, which narrows the cash gap from the inside.
  • Backlog turning into revenue: about $578B, with AWS growth above 40% as Jassy projects.
  • Memory prices cooling. Micron expects more moderate increases after its current quarter.
  • Lower long-term rates, since the supplier loan facility runs at variable rates.
  • Trainium taking more of the load, which lowers what each unit of capacity costs.

What would make it worse

  • A 2027 spending figure well above $220B, funded by more bonds. Its July $25B sale drew a cooler reception, with orders falling from a $62B peak to about $41B (Bloomberg Government).
  • The Fed hiking. A December hike is about 90% priced and the 10-year is near 5%.
  • Memory getting dearer. TrendForce (Sept 30) forecast fourth-quarter DRAM contract prices up 10–15% and NAND up 15–20%; Micron sees supply tighter in 2027 and 2028.
  • Either of the two big AI-lab customers slowing its commitments. Both are also companies Amazon has invested in.
  • The AWS margin slipping while interest expense keeps climbing.

How this company is placed against it

  • Credit is still strong: S&P AA with a stable outlook, though it noted "more pronounced erosion of financial flexibility" (The DESK, May 27).
  • It has the clearest balance-sheet strain of the big four: $115.1B of debt issued in nine months, a $17.5B term loan, and a secured supplier facility of up to about $80B that can be drawn from early 2027.
  • Against peers: Microsoft is building from cash flow; Amazon is not. Its cushion is a retail and advertising business that just posted a record operating margin.

Money plans

  • Capital spending: $77.3B in the quarter; about $220B for 2026; 2027 higher, no figure.
  • Debt: long-term debt $173.1B; $48.1B issued in Q3; supplier facility of up to about $80B from 2027.
  • Buybacks and dividend: none.
  • Investments: $50.0B in OpenAI preferred stock.

People and governance

  • CEO: Andy Jassy, since 2021. Not the founder.
  • CFO: Brian Olsavsky, since 2015.
  • Successor named: no. The proxy says the board reviews CEO succession at least once a year.
  • Control: one share class. Founder Jeff Bezos is executive chair and holds 8.8% (2026 proxy). Chair and CEO are split; the lead independent director is Jamie Gorelick.
  • Recent changes: AWS senior vice president Dave Brown left at the end of July 2026 after 19 years and Dave Treadwell took over (Reuters via Star-Advertiser). Investigations: not found.
  • How much rests on one person: not much. The founder stepped back five years ago and still chairs the board, the CEO and CFO are long-serving, and no one holds voting control.
