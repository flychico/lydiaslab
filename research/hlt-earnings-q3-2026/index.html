HLT earnings — Hilton Worldwide, Q3 2026

October 27, 202612 min readOpen in the reader

Management sees steady fee growth from business travel and record hotel openings, and the thing that could spoil it is a Middle East that stays shut while 2027 has to beat a World Cup year.

Reported 2026-10-27. Next report: not found. Updated 2026-11-12. Research, not investment advice.

The short version

  • Demand: Stronger in the US and Europe, weaker in the Middle East and China. Business travel and groups each rose about 8%; leisure rose 1.6%.
  • Guidance: Raised on RevPAR (room revenue per available room) to 4.0% to 4.5% from 3.0% to 3.5%, and on adjusted EPS to $9.07 to $9.13 from $8.89 to $9.01. The adjusted EBITDA outlook of $4,060M to $4,080M was trimmed for the Middle East and timeshare fees.
  • Biggest worry: The Middle East conflict, plus a slower 2027 (RevPAR growth of roughly 3% to 4%).
  • Chart: Leans up. A daily close below $359.41 ends that.

The numbers

  • Revenue $3,325M against about $3.37B expected: a miss. A year ago it was $3,120M.
  • Adjusted EPS $2.45 against $2.36 expected: a beat, adjusted against adjusted. GAAP EPS was $2.37; do not set that one against the estimate.
  • RevPAR up 5.8%. US up 7.0%, Europe up 6.5%, Middle East and Africa down 5.6%, China down about 5%.

What management said about the road ahead

  • Next quarter: RevPAR up 3.0% to 3.5%, adjusted EPS $2.32 to $2.38. The US midterm elections are named as a drag.
  • 2027: An informal view from the call of roughly 3% to 4% RevPAR growth. The World Cup helped Q3 and makes next year's comparison harder.
  • Who is traveling: Middle-income and mid-scale demand is recovering faster than luxury is growing.
  • Openings: A record 182 hotels and 27,000 rooms in the quarter. "We achieved our highest quarter ever for organic openings" (Christopher Nassetta, President and CEO, press release).
  • Pipeline: 543,100 rooms, construction starts up 40%, net unit growth of 6% to 7%.

What management is worried about

  1. The Middle East conflict: regional RevPAR down 5.6% and a trimmed EBITDA outlook.
  2. A tougher 2027: RevPAR growth slowing to roughly 3% to 4% against a World Cup year.
  3. Timeshare licence fees below plan (no figure given).
  4. China down about 5%, and weakness in Mexico.
  5. Higher rates slowing development financing in Europe.

The big worry: the Middle East and a tougher 2027, both ways

What would ease it

  • The Strait of Hormuz reopening. Exxon's CEO expects it to reopen but gave no date.
  • Airlines returning to the Gulf. A Briefs Finance report (Oct 3) said British Airways and Lufthansa planned to restart Dubai service this autumn.
  • The region's busy season. The same report cites analyst projections that Hilton's Middle East RevPAR in Q4 runs about 65% above Q2.

What would make it worse

  • A longer conflict. STR forecasts Q4 RevPAR in Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Jeddah and Riyadh about 30% below last year, and does not see the region back at 2025 levels until 2028 (as reported by Briefs Finance).
  • A weaker US base year than Hilton assumes. CoStar and Tourism Economics forecast US industry RevPAR up 4.4% in 2026 but only 2.1% in 2027, with June and July dragging on room rates. That is a US industry figure, not Hilton's, and the page carried no date.
  • The Fed. A December hike is priced and Dallas Fed President Logan names hotel stays among the sticky service prices it wants to cool.

How this company is placed against it

  • It collects fees and owns little: capex and contract costs are only $350M to $375M this year. A soft region trims fees; it does not leave Hilton holding empty buildings.
  • The US and Europe, growing 6.5% to 7.0%, carried the quarter. Visa also flagged the Middle East for cross-border travel, so this is not a Hilton-only problem.
  • Debt is $13.5B at 3.2x net leverage, with the 10-year near 5%.

Money plans

  • About $3.5B returned to shareholders this year; $2.9B done through October, $845M of buybacks in Q3.
  • Quarterly dividend $0.15.
  • Capex and contract costs $350M to $375M; debt $13.5B.

People and governance

  • CEO: Christopher Nassetta, President and CEO since 2007. Not a founder.
  • CFO: Kevin Jacobs, CFO since 2013, at Hilton since 2008.
  • Chair: Jonathan Gray, non-executive chairman since 2007, so the chair and CEO roles are split. Lead independent director: Douglas Steenland (per a third-party summary of the 2026 proxy).
  • Successor named: No. Skift reported in September 2025 that Nassetta plans to stay "for the foreseeable future" while preparing several internal candidates.
  • Control: Dual-class shares or a controlling holder: not found on the pages opened.
  • Recent changes: Hotel Dive reported (May 6, 2026) that brands chief Chris Silcock retires in Q1 2027, Laura Fuentes becomes chief brand officer and a chief technology officer role was created. No investigation found.
  • How much rests on one person: A lot. Nineteen years under one CEO with no named successor, eased by a long-serving CFO and a separate chair.
