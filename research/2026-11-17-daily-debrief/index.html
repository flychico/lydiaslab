Lesson of the day

Time decay: why the DAL call is bleeding faster now

Every option you buy has two prices inside it: what it's worth if the stock stopped moving today (intrinsic value, zero for an out-of-the-money call), and what you pay for the chance it moves before expiry (time value). Theta is how much time value drains away each day. It isn't steady. It speeds up as expiry gets close.

The DAL Dec 18 $90 call cost $632.50 on Nov 9 with DAL at $92.25. Tonight it's $200.50 with DAL at $84.30. Most of that $432 loss is the stock falling. But run the same contract with DAL frozen at $84.30 (Black-Scholes, its own 41% IV):

So if DAL just sits here, the call loses about a third of its value in ten trading days without the stock doing anything. That's why our rule sells 7 trading days before expiry (Dec 9 for these), and why we now prefer Dec–Jan contracts with delta 0.40–0.60: closer to the money, more of the price is real value, less of it is melting time.

Fib in action

Fib in action: clusters

A cluster is when levels from different swings land on nearly the same price. One Fib level is a guess; three from three swings is a crowd of traders all watching the same spot.

SPY has one right overhead. Swing 1, the big run (Mar 30 low $626.11 → Oct 6 high $781.62, $155.51): 23.6% = 781.62 − 155.51 × 0.236 = $744.92. Swing 2, the first drop (Oct 6 → Nov 5 low $732.71, $48.91): 23.6% = 732.71 + 48.91 × 0.236 = $744.25. Swing 3, the full drop to today's low (Oct 6 → $727.00, $54.62): 38.2% = 727.00 + 54.62 × 0.382 = $747.87. Three levels in a band 0.5% wide, on top of the old $744–745 floor.

What price did: Monday opened $749.66, right above the band, and fell through it to close $739.95. Today's high $739.76 never got back near it. Old floor, new ceiling.

The trade: no SPY call until a close above $748. If the gap stalls inside $744–748 and closes back under $739.89 (23.6% of the drop), that's the confirmation for the put plays; a stop belongs just above the band, at $749, not on it.
