Management sees years of growth from piping gas to power plants and data centers, and the thing that could spoil it is spending at a record pace on borrowed money while rates rise and Texas turns against new data centers.

The short version

  • Demand: Stronger. Record volumes in Texas gas transport and NGL exports; more than 9.5 million MMBtu a day of new agreements with data centers and power plants since the start of 2025.
  • Guidance: Raised. 2026 Adjusted EBITDA $19.7–19.9B, was $18.8–19.1B. About $285M of the raise is a one-off.
  • Biggest worry: Peak spending on a big debt load. About $7B of growth capital in 2027 against $68.85B of debt.
  • Chart: No lean. A daily close above the 50-day at $20.14 leans up; a daily close under the Nov 9 low of $18.79 leans down.

The numbers

  • Net income per unit $0.33 against about $0.41 expected: a miss.
  • Revenue $37.1B against $32.4B (Webull) or $35.6B (Earnings Whispers). Adjusted EBITDA $5.03B, up 31%.
  • The miss is on GAAP earnings per unit; the raise is on Adjusted EBITDA, the figure this business steers by. They are different measures, so a miss and a raise in the same report is not a contradiction.

What management said about the road ahead

  • Bookings: More than 9.5 million MMBtu a day of agreements with data centers, power plants and similar customers since the start of 2025.
  • A named-size deal: A 10-year, 250,000 MMBtu a day contract with a highly rated cloud company to supply a 900 MW AI campus expansion in Abilene, Texas.
  • Spending: 2027 growth capital about $7B, probably the high-water mark, then more than $5B a year for several years. Projects are expected to return in the mid-teens.
  • Contracts: "The majority of these growth projects are contracted under long-term commitments." (Tom Long, Co-CEO)
  • Trading gains: About $1.5B in 2026 against a plan of $200–400M, helped by higher prices and volatility. Part of that will reverse; sources disagree on how much.
  • Volumes: Records in Texas gas transport and NGL exports; some crude pipeline volumes fell.
  • Deals: Buying Vaquero Midstream for about $2.63B in cash and units, closing in Q4.

What management is worried about

  1. Project execution on a spending plan that rises from $5.4–5.6B in 2026 to about $7B in 2027.
  2. Customer credit on data-center deals. Named on the call; no figure given.
  3. Approval of the Vaquero deal. About $2.63B.
  4. A federal pipeline-safety corrective order dated Sep 21, 2026, new in the 10-Q. Cost: not found.
  5. A tax exposure of up to about $346M on one pipeline, new in the 10-Q.
  6. Governance exemptions. One updated risk factor on Texas Stock Exchange governance exemptions.

The big worry: peak spending on borrowed money, both ways

What would ease it

  • The contracts hold. Management says most growth projects are under long-term commitments, with mid-teens returns.
  • 2027 really is the peak. Spending is guided to step down to more than $5B a year afterwards.
  • Rates top out. The Fed's September projections have the policy rate drifting down from 2028, and Fed staff expect inflation to fall toward 2% next year as the energy and tariff shocks fade.
  • The Texas pause ends with clear rules. The current freeze on data center grid connections and environmental permits is tied to an audit expected to finish this year (Texas Tribune).

What would make it worse

  • More rate rises. Futures put about 90% odds on a December rise and better than even on January; the 10-year Treasury is near 5%. That raises the cost of every dollar ET refinances or borrows for the build.
  • Texas. Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa takes office Jan 19 and has promised a moratorium on all data center approvals until the Legislature passes protections (Texas Tribune). TheStreet reported a Goldman Sachs estimate that state and local moratoria could touch about 45% of planned national capacity, with their length the deciding factor. ET's headline contract is for an AI campus in Abilene, Texas.
  • Customer credit. The Fed's Bowman has flagged more than $800B of AI debt and circular financing as a risk. ET itself named customer credit on these deals.
  • The trading windfall fading. About $1.5B this year against a $200–400M plan; next year's comparison gets harder.

How this company is placed against it

  • Leverage, my rough arithmetic: $68.85B of consolidated debt against $19.7–19.9B of Adjusted EBITDA is about 3.5 times. Management's own leverage target: not found.
  • It is a toll road more than a price bet. Oil shocks helped 2026 through trading, but the growth case is gas volume under contract, which is why the oil producers' outlook matters less here than the Fed's and Austin's.
  • Versus peers in your docs: it is the only one of the energy names raising spending hard. Exxon and Chevron are holding capex flat and Occidental is paying down debt.
