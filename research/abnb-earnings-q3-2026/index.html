ABNB earnings — Airbnb, Q3 2026

November 5, 202612 min readOpen in the reader

Management sees strong demand in every region and raised its outlook again, and the thing that could spoil it is unrest in Europe and the Middle East landing on bookings that are now easier to cancel.

Reported 2026-11-05. Next report: not found. Updated 2026-11-12. Research, not investment advice.

The short version

  • Demand: Stronger. Nights and seats booked 155.0M, up 16%; first-time bookers up 13%.
  • Guidance: Raised. Full-year revenue growth in the high teens (was "at least mid-teens"), adjusted EBITDA margin about 36% (was at least 35.5%). Q4 revenue $3.22B to $3.28B.
  • Biggest worry: EMEA unrest, and cancellations rising to about 19% from about 16% as pay-later spreads.
  • Chart: Leans up, weakly. It sits just above $179.20; a daily close below $173.44 ends the lean.

The numbers

  • Revenue $4,869M, up 19%, against about $4.75B expected: a beat.
  • GAAP EPS $3.16 against a Webull estimate of $2.85. The basis of that estimate is unknown, so do not treat it as a clean beat.
  • Adjusted EBITDA $2,509M, a 52% margin. Gross booking value $27.7B, up 21%.

What management said about the road ahead

  • Next quarter: Revenue up 16% to 18%, nights growth of at least low double digits, booking value up mid-teens, adjusted EBITDA margin slightly below Q4 2025.
  • Regions: North America nights up low double digits, the best in four years. Latin America and Asia Pacific each above 20%.
  • Pay-later: "Reserve Now, Pay Later" added about 3 points to nights growth. A trade recap says the CFO called Q3 the peak of that benefit.
  • Supply: Listings are growing in the high single digits, slower than nights. Pricing tools for hosts are rolling out.
  • New lines: Hotels, services, car rentals and an AI trip-planning agent planned for the first half of 2027. No revenue disclosed. 2027 guidance: not found.
  • No quote used: none could be confirmed against the shareholder letter.

What management is worried about

  1. EMEA. The shareholder letter says the company is closely monitoring developments there. Recaps add that the Q4 forecast assumes no lasting impact from unrest in France or the Middle East conflict (not confirmed in the letter).
  2. Cancellations at about 19%, up from about 16% (secondary source).
  3. Listings growing slower than nights: high single digits against 16%.
  4. The filing's risk section: not found.

The big worry: EMEA unrest and pay-later cancellations, both ways

What would ease it

  • Calm in France and the Gulf through the winter booking season. Exxon's CEO expects the Strait of Hormuz to reopen, with no date.
  • EMEA holding its pace. RentalScaleUp reports EMEA nights grew in the mid-teens in Q3, with growth speeding up in France, Germany, Spain and Italy.
  • Europe's rental rules staying slow. The EU's Affordable Housing Act proposal (Sep 9, per Skift) still needs approval from member states and Parliament.

What would make it worse

  • The conflict biting again. Reuters reported in May that it cut about 1 point from Q2 nights growth, raised cancellations in EMEA and Asia Pacific, and was expected to stay a headwind in the second half.
  • The pay-later lift fading while cancellations stay. Without it, nights growth would have been about 13% (RentalScaleUp). More than 40% of booking value is now eligible.
  • Tighter rules. The EU proposal would give cities a legal path to restrict short-term rentals in areas under housing stress.

How this company is placed against it

  • Growth is spread out: North America at a four-year best, Latin America and Asia Pacific above 20%. EMEA can soften without sinking the quarter.
  • $12.6B of cash and investments (per research notes), with the 10-year near 5%.
  • Against peers, the same recap has Booking guiding room nights up 1% to 3% and Expedia booking value up 3% to 5%. Airbnb guides well above both.

Money plans

  • $0.7B of buybacks in Q3, $2.7B left, plus a new authorization of up to $12B.
  • No dividend. Capital spending: not found.

People and governance

  • CEO: Brian Chesky, co-founder, CEO and chairman of the board (2026 proxy). Founder-led. His start year was not on the pages opened.
  • Co-founders: Nathan Blecharczyk is Chief Strategy Officer and a director. Joseph Gebbia is a director, not an executive.
  • CFO: Elinor (Ellie) Mertz, CFO since March 2024.
  • Chair/CEO split: No. Chesky holds both; Kenneth Chenault is the independent lead director.
  • Successor named: Not found in the proxy sections read.
  • Control: Dual-class. Class B shares carry 20 votes, Class A one. As of April 8, 2026 Chesky held 31.9% of the voting power, Blecharczyk 31.1% and Gebbia 17.6%; officers and directors together 80.6%. A voting agreement binds the three to vote each other onto the board.
  • Recent changes: Chief Technology Officer Aristotle Balogh left his executive role in December 2025 (proxy). Shareholders were asked in June 2026 to vote on ending the dual-class structure; the board opposed it. The result: not found. No investigation found.
  • How much rests on one person: Most of it. Chesky is founder, CEO and chair, and the three founders can outvote everyone else.
