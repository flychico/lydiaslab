Beige Book — April 15, 2026

April 15, 20262 min readOpen in the reader

Official report: Summary · Full PDF Local PDF: 2026-04-15 Beige Book.pdf

Overall economic activity

Activity rose slightly to moderately in eight of twelve Districts, was flat in two, and fell slightly in two. Uncertainty from the Middle East conflict led many firms to take a wait-and-see approach. Manufacturing grew and banking was steady. Housing softened as mortgage rates rose, while commercial real estate improved, especially industrial and data center projects. Energy activity rose slightly, but producers were cautious about drilling. Agriculture was mixed. The report was prepared at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York.

Consumer spending

Consumer spending edged up despite winter weather and higher fuel prices. Lower-income households showed financial strain, while spending by higher-income households held up.

Labor markets

Employment was steady to slightly up, with one District reporting a slight decline. Layoffs were minimal. Firms hired mainly to replace departing workers and increasingly used temporary or contract staff. Labor availability improved, but shortages of skilled trade workers persisted. Most Districts saw no significant AI effect on staffing yet, though some firms had slowed hiring because of productivity gains. Wages rose modestly to moderately, with pressure in health care and the skilled trades.

Prices

Price growth was moderate overall. Input costs rose faster than selling prices, squeezing margins. Energy and fuel costs jumped in every District, raising freight costs and prices for petroleum-based products. Tariffs pushed up metal prices. Technology, insurance, and health care costs also kept climbing.

Notable themes

  • Middle East conflict: the main source of uncertainty and the driver of an energy/fuel cost jump.
  • Energy/oil: fuel costs up everywhere, feeding into freight.
  • Housing: softening as mortgage rates rose.
  • Data centers: supporting commercial real estate.
  • Tariffs: lifting metal prices.
  • AI: little staffing effect so far, some hiring slowdowns from productivity gains.

District highlights

  • Boston: Slight decline; flat jobs and wages; home sales slowed.
  • New York: Modest decline due to tariff shifts and the Middle East conflict; input prices rose sharply.
  • Philadelphia: Slight growth; manufacturing up moderately; uncertainty rose.
  • Cleveland: Modest growth; manufacturing demand up, retail down; housing rebounded.
  • Richmond: Modest growth; consumer spending up modestly.
  • Atlanta: Modest growth; real estate improved; energy up; agriculture flat.
  • Chicago: Slight increase; manufacturing up modestly; financial conditions tightened; 2026 farm income expectations fell.
  • St. Louis: Unchanged; cautious optimism; concern about energy costs.
  • Minneapolis: Slight increase; input price pressures intensified.
  • Kansas City: Slight growth; suppliers added fuel and logistics surcharges; oil and gas steady.
  • Dallas: Slight expansion; manufacturing growth slowed; outlooks worsened.
  • San Francisco: Stable at subdued levels; prices up on energy; agriculture down.

Read-through for SPY

  • An energy shock with input costs outpacing selling prices is a direct hit to corporate margins outside the energy sector.
  • Rising mortgage rates and tighter financial conditions in some Districts are a headwind for housing and rate-sensitive stocks.
  • Data center demand keeps showing up as a bright spot, supporting AI-linked names.
