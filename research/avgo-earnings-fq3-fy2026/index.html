AVGO earnings — Broadcom, fiscal Q3 FY2026

September 2, 202613 min readOpen in the reader

Management sees custom AI chip demand running ahead of supply for years, and the thing that could spoil it is that a handful of customers, some now financed with Broadcom's own backing, do not build everything they have announced.

This report is about 10 weeks old and the next quarter has already closed. Its guidance is replaced on Dec 9.

Reported 2026-09-02. Next report: Dec 9, 2026 (date from Webull, not confirmed elsewhere). Updated 2026-11-12. Research, not investment advice.

The short version

  • Demand: stronger. AI chip revenue $16.7B, up 221% from a year ago; management says demand is above its outlook and supply is the limit.
  • Guidance: raised. Fiscal Q4 revenue about $34.8B (the Q3 guide had been about $29.4B); fiscal 2026 AI revenue about $58B, from $56B. Operating margin guide one point lower, at about 66%.
  • Biggest worry: a few customers, partly financed by Broadcom itself. Top five end customers are about 55% of revenue, and a new financing backstop carries a maximum liability of about $29B.
  • Chart: no lean. Price closed $345.22, right on the 50-day ($345.49) and still under the 200-day ($366.22). A close above $366.22 decides it up; a close below $289.77 decides it down.

The numbers

  • Revenue $29.59B, up 86%, vs $29.43B estimated. A modest beat.
  • GAAP EPS $2.68 vs $2.56 estimated; non-GAAP EPS $3.32. The estimate is Webull's and analysts usually estimate non-GAAP, so this EPS comparison mixes GAAP and adjusted. Treat it as rough.
  • AI chip revenue $16.7B against a guide of $16.0B.

What management said about the road ahead

  • Next quarter: revenue about $34.8B, AI chip revenue $21.7B, non-GAAP operating income about 66% of revenue.
  • Further out: AI revenue of about $115B in fiscal 2027 and about $230B in fiscal 2028. These two figures rest on a single automated transcript; hold them loosely until Dec 9.
  • Supply: it says supply is secured for its fiscal 2027 and 2028 outlook. Supply, not orders, is the limit today.
  • Customers: six custom AI chip customers; custom chips were 73% of AI revenue.
  • Backlog: contracted revenue not yet recognized is about $179.2B, about a quarter of it due within 12 months (10-Q).
  • Margins: more custom chips with more memory in them pull gross margin down, so management points you to operating margin instead.
  • In their words: "Demand for our custom AI accelerators and networking continues to be very strong." (Hock Tan, President and CEO)

What management is worried about

  1. Customers not building everything they announced. Management cautioned that not all of the capacity customers have announced is guaranteed to be built in the period.
  2. Financing its own customers. A customer financing platform launched in June shows up in this filing for the first time, with a maximum backstop liability of about $29B. (That it is new is our reading; the filing does not label it.)
  3. Concentration. One distributor was 50% of revenue; the top five end customers about 55%.
  4. One manufacturer. TSMC made about 95% of its wafers.
  5. Margin mix. Operating margin guide down one point to about 66% as custom chips and memory content grow.
  6. Commitments. Purchase obligations of $126.8B, mostly due in 2027 and 2028, against $61.1B of debt principal.

The big worry: a few customers, partly on Broadcom's credit, both ways

What would ease it

  • The Dec 9 report holds or lifts the fiscal 2027 AI figure and names more than six custom customers.
  • The backstop stays near $29B instead of growing, and customers fund more with their own cash or public equity.
  • Suppliers keep confirming the build is real: TSMC raised 2026 growth to about 44% and reported October revenue up 59.7%; Micron holds $32B of customer commitments, mostly cash deposits.
  • The Fed stops hiking. Chair Warsh has said rates do bite on AI credit, so easier credit helps the buyers.

What would make it worse

  • The backstop grows fast. The Motley Fool reported on Oct 13 that Broadcom is guaranteeing $42B of senior debt for Anthropic and is in talks on up to $50B of financing for OpenAI, up to $92B in all. That article cites no filing for the figures, so wait for the 10-K.
  • A customer delays or resizes a site. With five customers at about 55% of revenue, one change shows.
  • Credit tightens: futures put about 90% odds on a December hike, the 10-year is near 5%, and Governor Bowman has flagged over $800B of AI debt and circular financing as a risk.
  • TSMC's caution proves right. It called its 2027 planning prudent and gave no 2027 spending figure.

How this company is placed against it

  • Protection: about $179.2B of contracted revenue and secured supply for two years. It is also paying down its own debt: $5.6B repaid in the quarter.
  • Exposure: it has committed $126.8B to suppliers on the strength of those few customers, and now stands behind some of their borrowing. If a customer stumbles, Broadcom is hit as supplier and as guarantor.
  • Versus peers: Nvidia's guarantees (up to $108.5B) are larger, but Nvidia sells to far more buyers. Broadcom's bet is narrower.

Money plans

  • Dividend: $0.65 a quarter; $3.1B paid in the quarter.
  • Buybacks: none in the quarter; $10.1B of authorization left.
  • Debt: $5.6B repaid; $61.1B of principal outstanding.
  • Spending: capital spending $532M (it does not own the factories); purchase obligations $126.8B. Hiring: not found.

People and governance

  • CEO: Hock Tan, 74, President and CEO since March 2006. Whether the company counts him as a founder: not found in the proxy. No fixed contract term found, but the board granted him a stock award in September 2025 that it says is meant to keep him leading through fiscal 2030.
  • CFO: new this year. Kirsten Spears retired effective June 12, 2026 and consults until March 15, 2027. Amie Thuener, 51, formerly Alphabet's corporate controller and chief accounting officer, took over June 12 (8-K, April 2). The Dec 9 report is among her first.
  • Successor named: no. The proxy says the board reviews a long-term CEO succession plan each year and keeps an emergency plan using an internal candidate. No name is given.
  • Chair: separate from the CEO. Henry Samueli, chairman since 2018, is classed as independent. Lead independent director Eddy Hartenstein retired at the April 2026 meeting and the board went from nine to eight.
  • Control: no dual-class shares or controlling holder mentioned in the proxy. Samueli has pledged about 0.3% of shares outstanding.
  • Investigations: none found.
  • How much rests on one person: a lot. A 74-year-old CEO of twenty years, no named successor, and a first-year CFO at the moment the company starts guaranteeing customer debt. That is the people risk to watch.

What would change this read

  • The 10-K: how much customer debt Broadcom now guarantees, and for whom.
