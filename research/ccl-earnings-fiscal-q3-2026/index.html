CCL earnings — Carnival Corporation, fiscal Q3 2026

September 29, 202613 min readOpen in the reader

This report is old: the quarter ended Aug 31, and it is about to be replaced by the December report, which brings the first full 2027 guide.

Management sees 2027 about half booked at record occupancy and price, and the one thing that could spoil it is marine fuel, because Carnival does not hedge.

Reported 2026-09-29. Next report: December, date not found. Updated 2026-11-12. Research, not investment advice.

The short version

  • Demand: Stronger. 2027 about half booked at record occupancy and price; customer deposits a Q3 record of $7.6B.
  • Guidance: Raised slightly. Full-year adjusted EPS about $2.24, up $0.02. Q4 adjusted EPS about $0.20, under the $0.24 consensus.
  • Biggest worry: Unhedged marine fuel. About $2.25B for the year; Q4 guided at $896 a metric ton.
  • Chart: Leans up. A daily close under $27.56 would end that.

The numbers

  • Revenue $8,435M against $8,153M a year ago and $8.36B expected. A beat.
  • GAAP EPS $1.40, adjusted EPS $1.43, flat on last year despite about $0.10 of fuel and currency drag. The beat is adjusted against adjusted: $1.43 against consensus of $1.36. GAAP $1.40 clears it too, so nothing is being mixed.
  • Fuel cost per ton was $826 against $607 a year ago.

What management said about the road ahead

  • Next quarter: Q4 adjusted EPS about $0.20, net yields up about 1.7%, fuel $896 a metric ton, capacity down 0.1%.
  • Full year: Net yields up about 2.3% in constant currency (raised), costs excluding fuel up about 2.2%, adjusted EBITDA about $7.14B.
  • Bookings: 2027 about half booked, occupancy and price at records. 2028 has started ahead of a year ago on both.
  • Q1 2027: Still carries damage from this spring's booking disruption, mainly long-haul and exotic itineraries. Management separates that quarter from the rest of 2027.
  • Supply: 2027 capacity up only about 0.5%. Caribbean industry capacity growth is acknowledged as a pressure.
  • Quote: "We delivered another quarter of top and bottom-line records" (Josh Weinstein, CEO, press release).

What management is worried about

  1. Fuel, unhedged: about $2.25B for 2026; about $150M of extra cost absorbed this year.
  2. Q1 2027 weakness: from the spring booking disruption; no figure given.
  3. Loyalty-programme accounting: a 0.4-point yield headwind in 2027.
  4. Caribbean capacity: industry growth there; no figure given.
  5. EU emission-allowance costs: named with fuel; no figure given.
  6. A full Risk Factors comparison: not found.

The big worry: marine fuel, both ways

What would ease it

  • Calm in the Strait of Hormuz. Waller at the Fed says insurance and transfers there add $20 to $30 a barrel. Brent was about $92.5 on Nov 11, down from above $110 in late October.
  • Talks holding. Brent fell to $96.54 on Nov 9 on de-escalation hopes, and VLSFO supply improved in Fujairah (ENGINE).
  • Refineries returning. Chevron says about 10% of global refining is offline and expects margins to ease after the near term.
  • With no hedges, every dollar of a fall reaches Carnival at once.

What would make it worse

  • More incidents in the Strait. A tanker hit a mine there on Nov 11 and WTI closed near $87.
  • A slow fix. Exxon's CEO expects product-market normalisation to run well through next year.
  • Distillate staying scarce. On Sept 30 the 20-port average was $860.50 a ton for VLSFO and $1,522 for marine gasoil; Fujairah stocks were about half the pre-conflict level and China suspended most October fuel exports (ShipUniverse, Oct 1).
  • No supply response. None of Exxon, Chevron or Occidental is raising spending. Occidental plans on $50 WTI and hedges with collars, so even a producer is not betting the shock lasts.

How Carnival is placed against it

  • Most exposed of your cruise names. Royal Caribbean is hedged 58% for 2026 and 53% for 2027; Carnival is at zero.
  • It covered this year's roughly $150M of extra fuel with more than $150M of operating gains and still raised the guide by $0.02. That is thin cover.
  • The balance sheet is better than it was: debt $23.9B, down from $26.6B, a second investment-grade rating, no secured debt.

Money plans

  • Nearly $1.2B of buybacks year to date and $618M of dividends; about $2B combined expected for 2026.
  • Total debt $23.9B, down from $26.6B. A December Fed hike is priced, which matters for refinancing.
  • Q4 capital spending about $1.2B. The next new ship class arrives in 2029.

People and governance

  • CEO: Josh Weinstein, since August 2022 (MarketScreener lists July 31, 2022). Not a founder; he was chief operations officer and followed Arnold Donald.
  • Chair: Micky Arison, listed as Executive Chair, son of founder Ted Arison. Chair and CEO are split, but the chair is an executive from the founding family.
  • CFO: David Bernstein, CFO since 2003, age 68 per MarketScreener. A long tenure with no successor named.
  • Successor named: not found, for CEO or CFO.
  • Control: The Arison family and trusts hold about 6.8% of combined voting power, with the Arison Group at 7.6% of Carnival Corporation (Panabee summary of the 2026 proxy, AI-generated; treat as approximate). Dual-class shares: not found.
  • Structure change: The dual-listed structure is gone. Holders approved unification on Apr 17, 2026, and it completed on May 7, 2026: one company, Carnival Corporation Ltd, moved from Panama to Bermuda. Carnival plc became a subsidiary and left the London exchange.
  • Recent changes or investigations (Nov 2025 to Nov 2026): no officer or director change in the two filings read; all 11 directors were re-elected. No investigation found.
  • How much rests on one person: Less than at a founder-run firm. The founding family chairs it but holds a single-digit vote; the open question is the CFO's eventual handover.
