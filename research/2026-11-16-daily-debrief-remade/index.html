Lesson of the day

The close decides, not the cents at the intraday read

KO traded over its $99.30 trigger most of the afternoon, dipped to $99.26 when the intraday read was taken, then closed $99.48. The old rule said the intraday read decides, so the first version of tonight's report left the call out. That's like judging a race at the 90-metre mark.

Our triggers and stops are written as closes, because a close is the price the market agreed to hold overnight. So the rule now reads: if a trigger was within about 0.3% at the intraday read and the close went through it, we open at the closing mid. Four cents is 0.04%. The same goes for stops: a close through a stop closes the play.

What it costs to be strict about it: entered at $99.48, the risk to $97.38 is $2.10 and the reward to $102.64 is $3.16, exactly 1.5 to 1. In dollars, about $78 at the stop against about $200 at target 1.

Fib in action

When a 1.382 stops being a floor

Your PLTR extension: A $118.34, B $188.15, C $70.90, so the swing is B − A = $69.81. Each level is C + ratio × $69.81. 1.382: $70.90 + $96.48 = $167.37. 1.618: $70.90 + $112.95 = $183.84 (the printed values; recomputing lands a cent higher).

In a run, the 1.382 and 1.618 are where moves pause. On the way down they're the floors that get tested. PLTR stalled at the 2.0, lost the 1.618, and now has a 3-day close under the 1.382. When the 1.382 fails, the next line is the 1.0, the "equal move": $70.90 + $69.81 = $140.71. That's the put's target. The stop goes above the failed line at a real level: a close over today's high, $166.09.

Try it: work out your 0.786 ($70.90 + 0.786 × $69.81) and check whether it lines up with the spring 2026 lows on the chart above.
