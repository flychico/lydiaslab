Lesson of the day

Judge the trade by the stock, not by the option's red number

Your DAL $90 call lost 44% today ($5.05 to $2.81) while DAL itself fell 5.2%. That's leverage working in reverse. An option moves several times faster than the stock, so the option's P/L will always look scarier than the chart.

DAL today−5.2%$91.32 → $86.55
The $90 call−44%$505 → $280.50

The plan never said "sell if the call is down 50%." It said sell if DAL breaks $83, the 50% line of its run. DAL is $3.55 above that. So the question tonight is not "how much am I down?" but "is the reason I bought still true?" The uptrend is intact and the stop hasn't been touched. Two honest caveats: time is now working against the call (it loses about $5.60 a day to time decay), and the most you can still lose is the $280.50 that's left. That's why we size every play so that losing all of it doesn't hurt the account.

Fib in action · Wick or break?

Nvidia stabbed through two Fib levels and closed back above them

A level only "breaks" when price closes through it. An intraday stab that closes back on the other side is a wick: sellers pushed, buyers pushed harder. Today Nvidia gave us the cleanest example of the year.

The levels: your 0.5 extension, $129.31 + 0.5 × $52.05 = $155.33. My downside extension of the October slide (A Oct 6 $243.37, B Oct 23 $210.18, C Oct 28 $219.99, A − B = $33.19): 2.0 = 219.99 − 33.19 × 2 = $153.61. Two ladders, drawn differently, landing $1.72 apart: that's a cluster.

What price did: opened $151.01, low $148.80 ($6.53 under your 0.5), close $166.19, $10.86 above it. Both levels = WICK. Last night's try-it answers: 2.382 = 219.99 − 79.06 = $140.93, 2.618 = $133.10; the low stopped $7.87 above the 2.382. For a trade: a put holder takes profit in a cluster like this.
