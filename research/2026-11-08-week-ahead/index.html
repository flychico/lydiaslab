Lesson of the week: Cheap or expensive: comparing the price tag with how much the stock really moves

Every option has an implied volatility (IV) built into its price. It's the market's guess of how much the stock will move in a year. Realized volatility is what the stock has actually done, here measured over the last 60 trading days. Divide the first by the second and you know if you're overpaying: under 0.9 is cheap, 0.9–1.25 is fair, over 1.25 is expensive.

  • NOW Dec $102 put: ratio 0.61 — IV 50% vs moves 82% → CHEAP
  • NVDA Dec $170 put: ratio 1.45 — IV 59% vs moves 40% → EXPENSIVE, earnings inside

Why it matters: when an option is expensive, the stock has to move more just for you to break even. NVDA puts are priced for a bigger move than NVDA usually makes, and they include its Nov 17 earnings, so that extra premium vanishes after the report (IV crush). Cheap doesn't mean good, either: NOW puts are cheap because the stock is already down a lot and RSI is 28. The price tag is one check, not the reason to trade. This week's four plays are all FAIR, between 0.92 and 1.20.

Fib lesson of the week (Fibonacci Playbook): Runaway extensions: how your XOM drawing called the top and mapped the drop

Last week there was no chart assignment to check, so we start fresh. It's an extension week.

A 3-point extension projects a past swing forward. You take one move (A to B), then measure it again from a new point C: level = C + (B − A) × ratio. In your XOM drawing the swing is $20.42 (roughly the late-April low near $143.6 to the May high near $164.0) and C is $126.05. Please confirm your exact A/B/C dates next time.

The math: 3.0 → 126.05 + 3 × 20.42 = $187.31. 2.618 → 126.05 + 53.46 = $179.51. 2.382 → 126.05 + 48.64 = $174.69. 2.0 → 126.05 + 40.84 = $166.89. 1.618 → 126.05 + 33.04 = $159.09.

What price did: the big ratios (2.382 to 3.0) are where runaway moves run out of buyers. On Oct 30 XOM hit $188.50 and closed at $187.25, right on the 3.0. Then it closed under 2.618 on Nov 3 ($177.46), under 2.382 on Nov 4 ($169.27) and under 2.0 on Nov 5 ($165.30). Friday's high of $166.79 failed ten cents under the 2.0: old support turned resistance.

What it means for a trade: as long as XOM stays under $166.89, the next stops down are $159.09 and then $154.3, where the 1.382 meets the 200-day. That confluence is a buy zone to study, with the stop just beyond it, a close below about $152. A close back above $166.89 flips it; that would point to the Dec $170 call ($485, OI 9,861, IV 30% vs moves 27% → FAIR) aiming at $174.69. Neither is a play yet.
