The vote flipped the board: travel up, oil and AI down.

Democrats won both the House and the Senate, and the market rallied, but not evenly. Your travel names ran, and the AI and oil names got hit. Full page: Debrief Nov 4

The one thing

SPY gapped out of its box and closed at $766.81. But it touched $775.64 and gave back $9. Sellers are waiting at $774–776. Hold $762, and the pullback is probably over.

The S&P 500 rose 1.6% to 7,688.62, the Dow rose 3.0%, and the Nasdaq rose 1.4%. The VIX fell about 20% to near 17. Oil fell hard (WTI about $89, Brent below $100), so energy fell while airlines and cruise lines rallied. The 10-year yield dipped to about 4.89%. AI infrastructure sold off on data-center fears (DELL −8.8%, MU −7.0%, NVDA −3.2%). Money went to Amazon (+6.1%) and AAPL (+5.3%) instead. Defense fell on budget worries (LMT −6.9%). Qualcomm fell about 6.6% after hours on its report. ADP showed +86K private jobs in October.

Lesson of the day: the trigger is your seatbelt, and it cuts both ways

A trigger is the exact price event you decide on before entering. You buy the confirmation, not the idea.

  • The NVDA Dec $220 call wasn't taken because its trigger (a close above $210.94) never fired. It went from $7.44 to $5.15 (−31%), so we avoided about a $230 loss.
  • The RCL Dec $340 call was missed because our dip to $307–308 never came. It went from $8.00 to $16.25 (+103%).

A missed winner costs $0. A forced entry costs real money. Don't chase RCL just because it left without you. See Options Playbook.
