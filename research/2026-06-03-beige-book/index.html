Beige Book — June 3, 2026

June 3, 20262 min readOpen in the reader

Official report: Summary · Full PDF Local PDF: 2026-06-03 Beige Book.pdf

Overall economic activity

Activity rose slightly to moderately in ten of twelve Districts, fell slightly in one, and was unchanged in another. Manufacturing grew in nine Districts. Banking conditions were mostly stable, though delinquencies on residential mortgages, consumer loans, and agricultural loans rose in several Districts. Agriculture was flat or weaker, and energy activity was cautious given uncertainty. Business outlooks were largely unchanged. The report was prepared at the Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City.

Consumer spending

Spending was mixed and increasingly split by income: higher-income households held up, middle-income households were cautious, and low-income households were under more strain. Credit card use rose, retail visits fell, and demand for necessities grew. Auto dealers saw softer new-vehicle demand and a shift toward used and hybrid models.

Labor markets

Employment was flat in eleven Districts and grew modestly in one. Manufacturing hiring was strongest in several Districts, aided by defense work and data center demand. Wage growth was modest to moderate and roughly kept pace with inflation, with more frequent wage adjustments and cost-of-living increases. Hiring was selective, mostly for critical roles or replacements, and workers were reluctant to switch jobs.

Prices

Prices rose at a moderate to strong pace, and most Districts reported higher inflation than in the prior report. Energy costs linked to the Middle East conflict were the main driver, spreading into shipping, packaging, groceries, and fertilizer. Non-labor input costs outpaced selling prices, squeezing margins, and firms' ability to pass on costs varied by sector. Firms responded with supply-chain changes, product adjustments, smaller offerings, and temporarily absorbing costs.

Notable themes

  • Energy/oil (Middle East conflict): the main inflation driver, now spreading into food and shipping.
  • Income split among consumers: strain at the low end, rising credit card use.
  • Credit quality: delinquencies rising on mortgages, consumer, and farm loans in several Districts.
  • Defense and data centers: supporting manufacturing hiring.

District highlights

  • Boston: Slight growth; flat employment; higher costs; mixed outlook.
  • New York: Slight growth after weakness; strong manufacturing; rising input prices.
  • Philadelphia: Slight decline in activity and employment; manufacturers' expectations rose.
  • Cleveland: Moderate growth; strong manufacturing and data-center-driven construction; robust cost and price increases.
  • Richmond: Modest growth across consumer, financial, and business services; moderate price growth.
  • Atlanta: Modest growth; travel slowed; real estate flat to down.
  • Chicago: Slight growth; prices rose rapidly; financial conditions tightened slightly.
  • St. Louis: Slight growth; robust price increases; slightly weaker outlook.
  • Minneapolis: Modest expansion; sharp price increases and high input pressures.
  • Kansas City: Slight growth; softer consumer demand; margin pressure.
  • Dallas: Modest growth; retail weakened; tepid outlook.
  • San Francisco: Stable; moderate price increases; weaker agriculture and residential real estate.

Read-through for SPY

  • Inflation re-accelerating on energy reduces the odds of rate relief, a headwind for valuations.
  • Input costs outpacing selling prices plus rising delinquencies point to margin and credit risk for consumer and financial stocks.
  • Broad growth (ten Districts up) and defense/data center demand keep the earnings floor intact.
