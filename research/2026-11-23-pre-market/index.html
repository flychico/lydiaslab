What's going on

1. The bounce: short covering with official help in Asia

The evidence: futures gained 2.7% (S&P) and 3.5% (Nasdaq 100) by 5:40 a.m., after the S&P fell 8.6% last week and closed in a correction. In Asia the help was explicit: Taiwan's regulator ordered tighter short-selling rules, China's exchanges announced support measures including easier margin financing, and Korea's finance minister repeated his plan to stabilise the market if necessary. No amounts were announced. The leaders are last week's losers: NVDA +7.6%, DELL +7.8%, CRDO +7.3%, BE +6.1%, HOOD +6.2%, SHOP +6.4%. Defensives barely move (KO +0.2%, XOM +0.9%).

What it means: when the most-sold names rise the most and the defensives sit still, shorts are covering; it does not mean buyers decided the AI story is fixed. Deutsche Bank's Jim Reid on last week: "This was no longer simply a tech selloff." A bounce that fixes a forced-selling problem can run a few days. What would prove me wrong: the gap fades and SPX loses 7,081 in the first hour. Then the bounce was only overnight positioning and 6,900 is back on the table today.

2. Rates: the haven bid left the bond market

The evidence: the 10-year rose 5bp to 4.96% with the short end leading; Germany's 10-year +2.2bp to 3.32%. A 25bp December hike is still about 96% priced, but traders cut their bets on anything bigger. Today: a $69 billion 2-year auction at 1:00 p.m. and Williams at 4:00 p.m.

What it means: rising short yields on a risk-on day is the market putting the Fed back at the centre now that panic is easing. Good for bank margins (JPM, BAC up 2.3–2.6%), bad for TLT and small caps over time. What would prove me wrong: a weak 2-year auction pushing yields up while stocks fall; then rates, not AI, become the problem into Wednesday's PCE.

3. Oil: attacks over the weekend, a shrug this morning

The evidence: several ships were attacked in the Gulf and near the Strait of Hormuz over the weekend, and the Houthis claimed an attack on a Saudi refinery Sunday (TipRanks). WTI $83.01 (−0.5%) and Brent $91.84 (−0.8%) anyway. AP: analysts expect oil prices to keep falling as supply stabilises; the US and Iran still have no permanent deal.

What it means: oil traders are treating each attack as noise while flows hold, which is why last night's "upside risk for Monday" did not show up. OXY $54.56 (+1.2%) is back over the 1.382 extension ($54.20) pre-market: the level that broke on Nov 19 is being tested from below. What would prove me wrong: Brent back over $95 on a real supply loss; then the OXY put idea is off and XOM leads.

And the deal: CrowdStrike for Zscaler

The evidence: CrowdStrike agreed to buy Zscaler in an all-stock deal valued at $43.9 billion, a 34% premium (Le Private Banker). Zscaler +12.8%, CrowdStrike −2.6% pre-market.

What it means: a buyer paying a premium for a software company in the week software lost 14% (NOW −14.3%) is a real data point that someone sees value. NOW is +3.5% pre-market. It is not a setup for us: NOW is still stretched with no level that gives room.
