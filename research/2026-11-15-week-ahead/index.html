The new backdrop: divided government from January

Democrats won both chambers on Nov 3 (about 228 House seats, about 54 in the Senate) and take over in January. The president has said he will veto their bills, so expect gridlock, oversight hearings and budget fights, not big new laws. Markets have usually handled gridlock fine.

The new part is AI. Data centers became an election issue. A national moratorium bill has long odds and a sure veto, but power sits with the states: Texas elected a governor who promised a pause on new data-center approvals, and Trefis estimates about 44% of planned US data-center power is in states that just elected Democratic governors. The day after the vote, the big buyers of computing (Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta) rose while power and neocloud names fell 7–14%; the next day chips cracked (SMH lost 11.2% that week). Delays push chip orders out; they rarely cancel them.

What it means for your themes: AI infrastructure now carries a timing risk (permits, power, states) on top of the rate risk, so size chip trades smaller. Defense and BA face budget fights, not new money. Travel and oil answer to Iran and fuel, not Congress.

Lesson of the week: The implied move: what the options say about Tuesday

Before a big event, you can read the market's own forecast straight off the option prices. Buy the at-the-money call and the at-the-money put together (a "straddle") and the total price is roughly the move the market expects, either way, by expiry.

Nvidia tonight: the Nov 20 $162.50 call costs $8.05 and the put $7.65. Together $15.70, or 9.6% of $162.85. So the market expects Nvidia between about $147 and $179 by Friday.

Here's the trap. If Nvidia moves $10 after the report, a call buyer picks the right direction and still loses, because the option priced in $15.70. The extra price is implied volatility (IV): 87% on these options versus the 44% the stock has actually been moving. That's why we call them EXPENSIVE (1.99). After the report the uncertainty is gone and IV collapses (IV crush).

The habit: before any event trade, compare the implied move with the move you expect. If you can't name a level the stock will reach beyond the implied range, the option seller has the edge, not you.

Plays to study

Cheap or expensive, in plain words: IV is how much the option price assumes the stock will move; realized volatility is how much it actually has moved over 60 days. IV ÷ realized under 0.9 = CHEAP, 0.9–1.25 = FAIR, over 1.25 = EXPENSIVE. An expensive option needs a bigger move just to break even.

A 3-point extension needs three points. A is where a move starts, B is where it ends, and C is where the pullback ends. The size of the first move (B − A) is then projected up from C. The 1.0 line is the "equal move": the second leg the same size as the first.

Your PLTR anchors (3-day candles): A $118.34 (Dec 27, 2024), B $188.15 (May 5, 2025), C $70.90 (Feb 12, 2025). The swing is B − A = $188.15 − $118.34 = $69.81. As you explained, you set that swing from the real trend, then slid the ladder so its lines sit on old turning points.
