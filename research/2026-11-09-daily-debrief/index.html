Lesson of the day: Why a winning call can outgrow the budget: delta

Delta is how much an option's price moves when the stock moves $1. A delta of 0.27 means the option gains about 27 cents per share (that's $27 per contract) for every $1 the stock rises. As the stock moves toward the strike, delta grows, so the option speeds up. That's the leverage that makes calls pay, and it works the same way in reverse.

  • SPY Dec $765 call, Friday: $638 (delta 0.27 · SPY $735.29)
  • Same call, today: $1,029 (delta 0.41 · SPY $752.69)

SPY rose 2.4%, and this call rose 61%. Its delta went from 0.27 to 0.41, so it now moves about $41 per contract for each $1 in SPY. The rally pushed it over the $800 limit before CPI gave us the green light. That's fine: the rule to wait for CPI was right, and we simply move up one notch to the $775 call ($611, delta 0.30). A lower delta costs less but needs a bigger move, so stay at or above 0.20.
