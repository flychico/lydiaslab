How NFL Point Spreads Work

A point spread evens out a game that is not even. Instead of asking who wins, it asks who wins by more than a set number of points.

Reading the line

Say the line is Chiefs -3.5 against the Falcons. The minus sign marks the favorite. The Chiefs must win by 4 or more for a Chiefs spread ticket to win. The Falcons are +3.5: they cover if they win outright or lose by 3 or fewer.

Half points (the .5) mean the game cannot land exactly on the number. When the line is a whole number, like 3, and the favorite wins by exactly 3, the result is a push and the stake is returned.

The price next to the spread

Most spreads carry a price of -110 on both sides. That means risking 110 to win 100. At that price a side has to win 52.4% of the time just to break even, because the sportsbook keeps a small cut on every game. That cut is why picking spreads at a 50% rate loses money over a season.

Why 3 and 7 matter

NFL scoring comes in field goals and touchdowns, so final margins bunch up at 3 and 7 far more than at any other number. A line moving from 2.5 to 3.5 changes a lot more than a line moving from 5.5 to 6.5. When you compare a model’s spread with the market, a half point across 3 or 7 is worth more than a half point anywhere else.

Why lines move

Lines open early in the week and move with injury news, weather and money from bettors. A line that moves a point and a half or more before kickoff usually means the market learned something.
