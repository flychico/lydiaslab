How Leo Reads an NFL Game

Every NFL game page on this site follows the same method. This page explains it once, so each game page can stay about its own game.

What Leo knows, and when the read freezes

Every stat on a game page above the Final result comes from games played before that game. Leo knows team results, quarterback play and the injury report as of its last read. It does not know game plans, weather changes or news after that read.

From early in the week Leo publishes an early read, which updates every morning and again on game day. Player props are added on game day. The last read saved before kickoff is frozen, and that frozen read is the one graded. Nothing is changed after the fact. Market numbers on a game page are the latest posted line before kickoff and the closing line after it. Nothing on the site is a wager.

The win chance

Leo’s win chance comes from four things: home field, the starting quarterbacks, each offense and each defense, all measured from games before this one. The starting quarterback comes from the last depth chart before kickoff, skipping anyone listed out or doubtful. The win chance adjusts for the starting quarterback only; other absences are listed for context and move the player projections. The “Why Leo has” section on each game page splits the win chance into those four parts in points.

The expected margin and the projected total come from separate models. In a close game the margin can point to a different winner than the win chance; the game page says so when it happens. What a win probability means.

The calls

Each game gets a moneyline and a spread verdict. Each is a call or a PASS, and only calls are graded.

  • Moneyline: a call when Leo gives a team more than 60% and the price is better than -230.
  • Spread: a call when Leo’s margin is at least 1 point off the line and his margin and win chance agree on the winner.

Calls are a public test, graded after every game: the moneyline at the price when Leo made the call, the spread at -110. The record is on Leo’s NFL record, with the reason for every miss.

Leo’s projected total is shown for information only. In past seasons taking a side of the total at any gap from the line came out about even, so from November 19 Leo shows the number and makes no total call.

The ratings

Each rating is 0 to 100 against the league this season, built from efficiency rather than record: 50 is average, and every 15 points is one standard deviation. Quarterback ratings use EPA, accuracy and giveaways; offense uses efficiency, explosive plays and ball security; defense uses EPA allowed, pressure and takeaways. The ratings describe the teams; the “Why Leo has” section says which of them push Leo’s number. The efficiency detail on each page shows the raw inputs so you can check the work.

Player projections

Each player projection is rate times volume times an opponent adjustment, and the tables show every input. Roles come from actual usage this season (most attempts, carries and targets), not the depth chart. Player projections are shown for information: Leo does not take a side of a posted prop line. After the game, each player miss is split into its two halves: how much the player was used and how efficient he was when he was. The main reason column names the half that was further off.

Touchdown chances

Each game page lists Leo’s chance that a player scores a rushing or receiving touchdown, the three most likely on each side, from the last read before kickoff. In 2023 to 2025, Leo’s top pick in a game scored 47% of the time, and at least one of its top two scored 69% of the time. These are chances, not promises.

The injury report

Teams file game statuses on the last practice day, and anyone not listed is expected to play. Final inactives come about 90 minutes before kickoff. How to read an NFL injury report.

Data

Player and team statistics come from the open nflverse dataset.
