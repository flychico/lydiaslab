How to Read an NFL Injury Report

Every NFL team files an injury report during game week. It is the most useful public information for a game, and it is easy to misread.

Practice status, Wednesday to Friday

For a Sunday game, teams list each injured player’s practice on Wednesday, Thursday and Friday:

  • DNP: did not practice.
  • Limited: practiced, but not fully.
  • Full: a full practice.

The trend matters more than any one day. A player who goes DNP, Limited, Full is usually on track to play. A player who goes Limited, Limited, DNP has a problem. For Thursday and Monday games the days shift to fit the schedule.

Game designations, on the final report

The last report of the week, usually Friday for a Sunday game, gives each player a game status:

  • Out: will not play.
  • Doubtful: unlikely to play.
  • Questionable: uncertain to play. Many questionable players do play, so this tag alone tells you little. Read it with the practice trend.

A player with no designation on the final report is expected to play. Teams also place players on injured reserve, which keeps them out for several games.

The last word: inactives

About 90 minutes before kickoff each team names its inactive players. This settles the questionable cases. Lines often move in the hour before kickoff for exactly this reason.

Which injuries move a game

A starting quarterback matters more than any other player, often by several points on the spread. After that, a few offensive linemen out together, a top pass rusher or a top cornerback can matter. One backup out rarely changes much.
