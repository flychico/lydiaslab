What a Win Probability Means

When Leo gives a team a 65% win chance, it does not mean that team will win. It means that in games like this one, that team should win about 65 out of 100 times. It should lose the other 35, and those losses are part of a good model, not proof of a bad one.

One game tells you almost nothing

A 65% team that loses has not shown the number was wrong, just as a coin landing tails twice does not show the coin is unfair. A probability is judged over many games: of all the teams Leo gave about 65%, did about 65% of them win? That check is called calibration.

Turning moneyline odds into a probability

Sportsbook odds carry a probability inside them.

  • Favorite at -150: divide 150 by 250 (150 plus 100). That is 60%.
  • Underdog at +130: divide 100 by 230 (130 plus 100). That is about 43.5%.

Add those two together and you get 103.5%, not 100%. The extra is the sportsbook’s cut. Taking it out (dividing each side by the total) gives the no vig chance: about 58% and 42% in this example. Leo’s no vig odds guide walks through the full calculation.

Comparing a model with the market

The market’s no vig chance is a strong estimate, because it reflects a lot of money and information. A model is useful when it is better calibrated than the market over a full season, not when it disagrees once. A gap of a few points between Leo’s chance and the market’s is normal.
