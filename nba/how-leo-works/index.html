How Leo’s NBA predictions work

Every NBA game page on this site shows one read from Leo: a win chance for each team and an expected margin, which is Leo’s own spread. This page explains what goes into that read, when it is saved, and how it is graded, so each game page can stay about the game itself.

When the read is saved

Leo reads every game several times on game day as the injury report changes. The read shown on a game page, and the one graded, is the last one saved before tip. Nothing written after tip changes it. If news breaks after the last read, the page says what Leo knew at the time, not what came out later.

What goes into the number

  • Team strength. How each team has done this season against the teams it played, blended early in the season with last season.
  • Home court. An average home edge, fitted on the last three seasons. Neutral site games are the one known gap.
  • Rest and back to backs. Days since each team last played, capped at four, and a penalty for a team that played the night before.
  • Missing players. The value of regulars on the injury report, described below.

The table “What moves Leo’s number” on each game page shows how many points each of these added to the home team’s expected margin. They add up to Leo’s margin.

How Leo counts injuries

A player marked counted on a game page is a regular whose value Leo has taken off his team: all of it when he is out, and 40% of it when he is day to day, because about 4 in 10 day to day players end up sitting. The minutes shown next to him are his average this season. Players without the mark play too little to move the number. Where a day to day player could still swing the game, the page says how far Leo’s number would move if he sits or plays.

What Leo knows and does not

Leo knows team results, rest and the injury report. It does not know matchups, lineups announced after the read, coaching decisions or motivation. The DraftKings line on each page is shown for comparison and is never an input. A gap between Leo and DraftKings is usually noise: in past seasons the side of the spread Leo liked covered about 51 to 52% of the time, so the gap is shown for information and is not a call.

The only official call is a Leo Pick: a team Leo gives 60% or more to win when the DraftKings moneyline on it is better than -230. Picks are graded at one price, locked at the first read that makes the pick, one unit each, on the results page.

Projected score

The projected score on each page splits Leo’s projected total around the spread shown, so the two always agree. It is information only. Leo makes no call on totals.

Box scores and the counted tag

After the final, each game page adds the ESPN box score saved the night of the game. Every player Leo counted as out or day to day is tagged with whether he actually played, so you can see when the injury report Leo read turned out wrong.

How a game is graded

After every night Leo is graded on three things: whether the team it gave 50% or more won, how far its margin was from the final, and each Leo Pick in units. A game counts as a miss when Leo had the wrong winner, missed the margin by 10 points or more, or lost a pick. Each miss gets one reason from the data, in this order: an injury input that was wrong, the market knew something (the line moved away from Leo and ended nearer the result), overtime, a blowout, variance (the line missed by about as much, within 3 points), or Leo’s own error. The game page shows that reason under What happened, and the results page keeps the running tally. For what a win chance means in general, see what a win probability means.
